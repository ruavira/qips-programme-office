**Key structural finding:** only **two** of the five health-quality credentials examined generate genuine recurring revenue from the credential itself — ISQua (annual) and NAHQ (biennial). ACHS and the Indian AHA programme are **one-off tuition businesses with no maintenance annuity**. This is the single most important design observation in Gap 1: most of the sector has *not* built the annuity, so building one is a differentiator rather than a norm to copy.

**NAHQ's bundling mechanic is the notable commercial design.** NAHQ prices recertification at **$0 for holders of its "CPHQ Package"**, which is **$705 USD for a 2-year subscription** (stated as including recertification cost for 2 years). Other NAHQ tiers: **Standard $319 USD / 1-year**, **Premium $1,299 USD / 1-year** (includes NAHQ Next registration). The effect is to convert a biennial transactional recertification fee into a continuous membership subscription — i.e. the credential is the hook, membership is the annuity.
Source: <https://nahq.org/membership/> · DOCUMENTED.

**ISQua's income-banded annuity is the most directly transferable model.** Nigeria is a World Bank lower-middle-income country, so the applicable ISQua figures are **€225 first year / €150 annual renewal**, inclusive of membership. Standalone ISQua membership is **€50/yr** (ISQua Member), **€150/yr** (Member + Fellow), **free** for "ISQua Friend" for users from low-income countries; student/retiree HIC €75/yr. Institutional membership prices did not render ("€ Loading…").

**Material inconsistency to be aware of:** the 2025 MGI figure reported as **74% first-year renewal** and the 2023 MGI figure reported as **43% first-year churn (≈57% renewal)** are irreconcilable as stated. Both come from secondary reporting of the same publisher's report series; I could not access MGI's primary report (registration-gated). Treat first-year renewal as **somewhere in the 57–74% band, uncertain**, and treat the **84% steady-state renewal** as the better-supported planning number.

**YOUR ARITHMETIC — ANCC implied renewal rate (illustrative only, do not quote as a renewal rate).**
Inputs: 344,572 active certificants; 45,139 renewals in 2025 (both from the ANCC PDF above). If ANCC certifications were uniformly on a 5-year cycle and the population were in steady state, renewals due per year = 344,572 ÷ 5 = **68,914**. Observed 45,139 ÷ 68,914 = **65.5%**.
Why this is not a renewal rate: ANCC's certificate pool mixes credential types with different cycle lengths; the pool is growing (28,626 new passes in 2025 alone), so the denominator is wrong; and renewal timing is not uniform. Presented only to show the order of magnitude is consistent with the 57–84% band above, not as a finding.

**The UMN structure is the single most useful precedent for SQHN's licensing leg**, because it demonstrates the three commercial variables a curriculum licence actually turns on: (1) **hosting** — licensor's server vs licensee's LMS, priced ~25% apart ($5,000 vs $4,000); (2) **granularity** — whole-programme vs per-module ($3,500/module × up to 7 modules = up to $24,500 if bought piecemeal, i.e. modular pricing is punitive relative to the $5,000 bundle); and (3) **renewal at 50% of initial** ($2,500 on $5,000; $2,000 on $4,000; $1,750 on $3,500) — a fixed, published annuity ratio.

**The ISQua EEA fee schedule is dual-purpose intelligence.** It is simultaneously (a) a **cost line** for SQHN if the programme seeks ISQua EEA accreditation (Nigeria = lower-middle-income = **€3,952 in 2026**, rising to €4,071 in 2027, payable **annually** across a 4-year award), and (b) the **exact commercial template** for how an accrediting body monetises "accrediting other people's training programmes" — income-banded, annual, multi-year-locked, with a cross-product discount. If SQHN's second revenue leg is accrediting Nigerian and West African training providers rather than licensing its own curriculum, this is the published model to copy.

**Project ECHO is the important counter-case.** The most successfully scaled health-education replication model in the world charges **nothing** for replication rights, monetising instead through grants and service contracts. Any business case that assumes licensing fees will materialise from African institutions should be tested against the fact that the global benchmark for this model is a **zero-fee IP licence with a mandatory training obligation**.

### 2.2 What curriculum licensing deals typically yield — the only sector-level benchmark found

**BIS Research Paper 194, "The value of Transnational Education to the UK"** (UK Department for Business, Innovation and Skills). **[STALE — 2012/13 data]**

- "The average annual fee accruing to UK higher education institutions across all modes and levels of transnational education delivery is approximately **£1,530 per active student**" (2012/13).
- Distance learning is the strongest revenue mode: "an average annual fee of **over £4,000 per enrolment at postgraduate level**."
- Crucially: "The fees per student associated with different **partnership arrangements (e.g. franchise and validation) are much lower** but at large scale can ensure substantial revenues." (The paper does not quantify how much lower.)
- Total UK TNE revenue 2012/13: **£495.8m**, of which postgraduate distance learning £184.2m (37.2%) and MBA programmes £186m. Business & Management masters were 18% of enrolments but **56% of revenue**.
- The paper "explicitly states it cannot provide reliable cost estimates or net value calculations due to limited institutional cost disclosure."

Source: <https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7df61440f0b6230268841e/bis-14-1202-the-value-of-transnational-education-to-the-uk.pdf> · DOCUMENTED · **[STALE]**

**Reading for SQHN:** the licensing/franchising leg is a **volume business with a low per-student margin**, and the UK government's own study could not establish its net value because institutions do not disclose costs. It is not a fast route to break-even for a 25–64-person first cohort.

### 2.3 Does train-the-trainer actually reduce cost per learner, or shift it?

**The strongest evidence found is a cluster randomised controlled trial — EVALUATED.**

Cost-effectiveness of train-the-trainer (TT) vs train-the-trainer-plus-expert-consultation (TT+) for implementing interpersonal psychotherapy, Type 2 hybrid effectiveness-implementation cluster RCT, urban schools, USA. Data collected 2016–2020; published 2024.

- Costs are reported **per cohort**, not per individual trainee: **TT $1,002 (SD 561)** vs **TT+ $1,431 (SD 325)**, in **2022 US dollars**. Difference **$429 (SE 169), p = 0.01**.
- 125 students across both conditions (~43 TT, ~82 TT+).
- Effectiveness: TT+ was **not better**. "Among the 109 students, the average improvement in MASC-2 scores for TT+ was **1.69 points less (i.e., worse)** than the average improvement for TT."
- Conclusion: "There was a **greater than 80% chance that TT was a good value** compared to TT+ for all values of willingness to pay."

Source: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10809609/> · **EVALUATED (cluster RCT)**

**What this does and does not establish.** It establishes, at RCT strength, that **adding expert consultation on top of a train-the-trainer structure costs 43% more and buys nothing** — i.e. the marginal expert-hour is the expensive, low-yield ingredient. It does **not** establish that TTT reduces cost per learner *at scale* in a health-quality programme in Africa. That specific question — **does the cascade actually lower unit cost, or merely relocate it onto downstream institutions** — remains **NOT FOUND**; the two candidate papers were inaccessible (see blocked register).

**Corroborating structural evidence from the cost side (see Gap 3):** in the Nigeria/Kenya/Tanzania blended-learning costing, **the face-to-face component accounted for 67–87% of total blended-learning implementation cost**, and **per-diems/accommodation/allowances were 30–40%** of implementer direct cost. This is the mechanism by which TTT can genuinely reduce cost per learner — by removing central per-diem-bearing face-to-face days — and equally the mechanism by which it merely shifts cost, if downstream institutions still have to fund the same per-diems locally. Source: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-024-06633-2> · EVALUATED.

### 3.1 Cost per participant — blended health professional training in Africa (the central finding)

**"Cost analysis of a training course to improve antenatal care in Kenya, Tanzania and Nigeria," BMC Medical Education, published 8 January 2025. Costs in 2021 USD. n = 89 healthcare professionals. EVALUATED (costing study alongside a feasibility study).**

Modality: self-directed learning (16 h) + facilitated virtual sessions (2.5 h over 3 days) + **2-day face-to-face sessions**.

| Site | Blended learning — **total** cost/participant | Blended learning — **implementer-only** cost/participant | Face-to-face comparator (5-day) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nigeria (Oyo)** | **USD 860** | **USD 759** | **USD 909** (35% higher than BL) |
| Kenya (Vihiga) | USD 860 | USD 759 | USD 1,144 (83% higher than BL) |
| Tanzania (Dodoma) | USD 830 | USD 624 | — |
| Tanzania (Zanzibar) | USD 1,353 | USD 1,267 | — |

- **One-off design cost of the blended package: USD 21,261**, "mostly determined by labour costs for the design of course contents and for coordinating meetings."
- **Cost structure of implementer direct costs:** DSA / accommodation / other allowances **30–40%** across sites; labour **23–25%**; transportation **11–24%**; venue and catering; technology (airtime and data bundles) **11%** in Kenya.
- **The face-to-face component represented 67–87% of total blended-learning implementation cost.**
- All costs converted from local currencies to 2021 USD using World Bank rates.

Sources: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-024-06633-2> and the study summary at <https://cergas.unibocconi.eu/cost-analysis-training-course-improve-antenatal-care-kenya-tanzania-and-nigeria> · EVALUATED.

**Critical caveat — do not read this as a 12-month unit cost.** This programme is roughly a **5-day-equivalent** intervention (16 h self-directed + 2.5 h virtual + 2 days face-to-face). The **USD 759–860 per participant in Nigeria** is the cost of a short blended course, not a year-long programme with placements. What transfers is not the total, but (a) the **cost architecture** — allowances and travel dominate, technology is ~11%, design is a large one-off — and (b) the **direction and size of the blended-vs-face-to-face saving in Nigeria: 35%**.

**The two hard numbers are 15 (Southampton, institutional policy) and 7 (Maloney, empirical, web-delivered health professional course).** The gap between them is entirely explained by cost architecture: Southampton's 15 carries full university overhead against a 3-year rolling average; Maloney's 7 carries only course-level fixed cost of AUD $1,590 against a $250 fee. **The correct planning inference is not "7" or "15" — it is that the minimum viable cohort is a direct function of how much fixed overhead the programme is asked to absorb, and that the Southampton rule bakes in a 3-year establishment period before the threshold binds.** For a 25–64 first cohort, both thresholds are comfortably cleared on the fixed-cost test; the risk is in the variable/allowance-heavy cost block identified in Gap 3, not in the fixed block.

**Contribution-margin figure for cohort-based professional education: NOT FOUND.** Neither Pilbara nor ACU publishes one; the concept is discussed methodologically without numbers.

**The three findings that matter operationally.**
1. **69.5% completion on a 3-month blended course** is the best LMIC anchor found. A **12-month** programme is four times longer; the honest planning position is that 69.5% is an **optimistic ceiling**, not a central estimate, and no 12-month LMIC figure exists in the sources found.
2. **Attrition is stratified by profession.** In Guinea, doctors succeeded at 53–67% and nurses at 9% on the same course. If the SQHN cohort is multi-professional, blended completion will not be uniform, and headline completion will be driven by cohort composition as much as by programme quality.
3. **The Ethiopian data identify the binding constraint as workload, institutional ICT infrastructure and technical support — not learner digital literacy or internet quality.** That points retention investment at employer engagement and institutional support, not at learner-facing tech training.

### 4.3 Does paying upfront versus in instalments affect completion?

**Direct evidence: NOT FOUND.** No study was located linking instalment payment structure to completion in professional or health professional education. Searches run are listed in the register.

**What was found, and how far it goes:**

| Evidence | Finding | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Gourville & Soman, "Payment Depreciation: The Behavioral Effects of Temporally Separating Payments from Consumption," Journal of Consumer Research, 1998, 25(2):160.** <https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/25/2/160/1799502> | "A consumer will gradually **adapt to a historic cost with the passage of time, thereby decreasing its sunk-cost impact on the consumption of a pending benefit**." Illustrative: "Subjects who pay for their concert tickets **one day in advance are significantly more likely** to attend to their sunk costs and go to the concert than are those who paid **six months in advance**." Four experiments; the accessible summary reports **no attendance percentages**. | EVALUATED (experimental) **[STALE]** |
| **Michigan Ross / Coursera** — Goli, Chintagunta & Sriram; >70 courses from one large public university, data **2012–2016** | "The act of paying for the certificate and the motivation derived from wanting to earn the certificate lead to a **10–12% rise in student engagement**"; paid users "spent **10% more time** on the course portal." **Absolute completion percentages by paid vs free group are not stated in the accessible article.** <https://michiganross.umich.edu/rtia-articles/certificates-boost-mooc-completion-rates> | EVALUATED **[STALE]** |
| **Instalments are standard practice among the closest comparators** | **ACHS QIL (9-month)**: AUD $4,163.50 in full **or 5 monthly payments of AUD $693.92**. **AHA India PG Certificate (6-month)**: INR 45,000 **or 3 instalments of INR 15,000**. Neither publishes completion data by payment mode. | DOCUMENTED |

**The defensible inference, stated as an inference and not a finding:** the payment-depreciation literature implies that a **payment made close in time to the consumption occasion exerts more pull on attendance than a payment made long before**. Applied to a 12-month programme, that logic favours **instalments timed to module starts** over a single upfront payment, because it repeatedly re-establishes a fresh sunk cost across the year rather than allowing a January payment to psychologically depreciate by June. **This is a theoretical extrapolation from a 1998 consumer-behaviour experiment, not evidence about professional programme completion, and should not be presented to the Central Coordinating Committee as evidence.** The countervailing commercial consideration — that instalments create collection risk and allow mid-year walk-away — is not addressed by any source found.

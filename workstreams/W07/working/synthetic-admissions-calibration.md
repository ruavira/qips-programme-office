# W07 synthetic admissions calibration

Status: synthetic process test; no real applicant data and no approved decision threshold

Date: 2026-07-28

## Test purpose

The test asks whether the proposed rubric rewards evidence of practice readiness rather than
prestige, ability to pay or a conventional employment relationship. All cases are fictional.

## Formula

For each criterion, `criterion points = (rating from 0–4 / 4) × criterion weight`. The six
criterion points sum to 100. Reviewers score independently before seeing each other's result.

Weights: relevance 15; improvement challenge 25; initiative/follow-through 20; protected-time
and completion plan 15; reflection/learning readiness 15; contribution/equity impact 10.

Ratings appear in criterion order. Mean absolute reviewer difference is 5.63 points; the largest
difference is 7.50 points. These six cases are too few to approve a threshold or claim reliability.

## Findings

1. **Formula ambiguity was real.** The normalized formula must appear on every score sheet.
2. **Prestige control worked.** SYN-C remained weak despite role and institution signals.
3. **Employer-equivalent access worked.** SYN-B and SYN-D remained assessable, but reviewers need a common rule for conditional project-access evidence.
4. **Ability to pay stayed outside the score.** SYN-F did not improve because sponsorship was strong.
5. **Two anchor pairs overlap.** Improvement challenge versus initiative, and protected time versus general feasibility, produced the largest reviewer differences.
6. **Reasonable adjustment must be evaluated as the operating plan.** Reviewers should not score an unadjusted hypothetical version of the applicant.

## Required revisions before a larger pilot

- Define `improvement challenge` as importance, specificity, access and feasible scope; score past execution only under `initiative/follow-through`.
- Define the protected-time criterion as a credible calendar, live-session plan, observership route and responsible support/equivalent contact.
- Mark project-access evidence as `verified`, `conditional` or `not established` outside the numerical score.
- Add an application-level evidence sufficiency flag; a high score cannot replace missing identity, qualification or access verification.
- Train reviewers on four shared examples, then run at least twelve further synthetic cases spanning countries, sectors, career stages and access arrangements.
- Predefine the material-difference trigger; 10 points or a two-level criterion difference is the current test proposal, not an approved rule.

## Decision sensitivity

Using purely diagnostic bands of 70 or above, 55–69.99 and below 55, both reviewers place every
case in the same band. The bands are not an admissions recommendation. They demonstrate that the
current examples are not testing enough borderline cases; the next calibration set must concentrate
on totals between 60 and 75 and deliberately vary one evidence element at a time.

## Options

### Option A — eligibility plus rolling first-qualified offers

- **What it is:** applicants who meet the minimum requirements receive offers in completion order until capacity is reached.
- **Case for:** simple, quick and easy to explain.
- **Case against:** application timing and information access dominate selection; it cannot compare readiness when qualified demand exceeds capacity.
- **Cost:** lower reviewer burden but higher risk of an unbalanced or underprepared cohort.
- **Timing:** defensible only if verified demand is below capacity.

### Option B — eligibility, double scoring and moderated selection

- **What it is:** an administrative screen, two independent 0–4 evidence scores, reconciliation of material differences and a conflict-controlled panel applying approved tie-break and equity rules.
- **Case for:** transparent, auditable and aligned to completion of an applied programme.
- **Case against:** requires reviewer training, calibration, data controls and a published reconsideration route.
- **Cost:** two reviews and moderation per complete eligible application; volumes remain unknown until Q006 is answered.
- **Timing:** recommended for cohort one.

### Option C — employer-nominated institutional cohort

- **What it is:** participating organisations nominate employees against agreed role, project and protected-time conditions.
- **Case for:** strong workplace access, payment and transfer-to-practice support.
- **Case against:** organisations rather than the programme shape access; early-career, independent and between-role applicants are excluded; nomination can reproduce hierarchy.
- **Cost:** institutional agreements and account management, with lower open-market application processing.
- **Timing:** useful as one recruitment channel, not the sole cohort-one selection model.

## Working recommendation

Advance Option B to council testing and permit institutional nominations as one applicant source
under the same scoring standard. Do not set the final pass score or cohort cap until the rubric is
trialled, W01 personas are available and Q006 supplies the demand denominator.

## Scoring formula correction

For criterion weight `w` and reviewer rating `r` from 0 to 4, the contribution to a 100-point
total is `(r / 4) × w`. The earlier phrase “multiply by the weight” was ambiguous and could
produce a 400-point raw total. All future instruments must print the normalized formula.

## Unresolved benchmark questions

- The surviving official GenerationQ material does not expose its historic scoring rubric; QIPS must not claim to reproduce it.
- Q006 prevents responsible estimates of reviewer volume, offer yield and cohort size.
- W01 personas and W03 credential level remain required before final qualification/experience thresholds.
- Scholarship criteria, budget and separation from selection still require a W16/CCC decision.

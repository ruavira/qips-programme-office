## 1. What a clinical placement actually costs a host site, and who pays

### 1.1 United Kingdom — a published national tariff paid *to* the host

**DOCUMENTED.** England pays placement providers directly. Rates for **1 April 2025 – 31 March 2026** (GBP, plus Market Forces Factor):

| Tariff | Rate 2025/26 (GBP) | Uplift | Paid to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical (non-medical) placement — nurses, radiographers, physiotherapists, paramedics, pharmacists, clinical psychologists + 17 AHPs | **£5,866 + MFF per FTE** (includes £107 pre-inflationary uplift offsetting MFF changes) | 4.27% | Placement provider |
| Undergraduate medical placement (secondary, primary and independent settings — single harmonised rate) | **£35,891 + MFF per student** | 4.47% | Placement provider (NHS and private) |
| Postgraduate medical trainee placement | **£13,933 + MFF**, plus salary contribution (F1 = £20,652 national / £22,624 London) | 4.47% | NHS placement provider |
| Undergraduate dental placement | **£37,652 + MFF per student** | 4.47% | Clinical placement provider |
| Dental core training (new 2025/26, effective 1 Sept 2025) | **£6,207 + MFF** + salary £32,856 national / £34,499 London | — | Provider |
| Dental specialty training | **£13,933 + MFF** + salary £64,097 national / £67,302 London | — | Provider |

Source: [Education and training tariffs 2025 to 2026, GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/healthcare-education-and-training-tariff-2025-to-2026/education-and-training-tariffs-2025-to-2026) — accessed 1 Aug 2026.

NHS England states the AHP tariff is "an annual contribution to the funding of practice learning placement activity" and that providers "must demonstrate that any funding for clinical placements is being utilised for the delivery of such learning" — examples given include "funding resources for a library, setting up simulation centres or by creating dedicated AHP education teams." Source: [NHS England / HEE AHP Clinical Practice Placement Tariff](https://www.hee.nhs.uk/our-work/capitalahp/ahp-clinical-practice-placement-tariff) — accessed 1 Aug 2026. **DOCUMENTED.**

**Read-across for SQHN:** the UK number is the price of a year of supervised, assessed, hands-on training with clinical liability transfer. A 40-hour non-clinical observership is a fraction of that scope. The tariff is a ceiling for framing, not a benchmark to pay.

### 1.2 Australia (Victoria) — a *maximum chargeable* schedule, and it runs the other way

**DOCUMENTED.** In Victoria the education provider pays the health service, and the state caps what may be charged. From the *Standardised schedule of fees for clinical placement of students in Victorian Public Health Services*, effective for activity from **1 January 2025**, all figures **AUD, GST exclusive, per standard 7.6-hour clinical placement day**:

| Discipline | Supervision by health service | Supervision by education provider |
|---|---|---|
| Nursing and Midwifery | **AUD 71.45** per student per day | No fee |
| Allied Health | **AUD 40.16** per student per day | No fee |
| Paramedicine | **AUD 40.16** per student per day | No fee |
| Medicine | Annual fee based on EFTSL activity | No fee |

Medicine rates per EFTSL: Commonwealth-supported **AUD 1,746**; domestic fee-paying **AUD 4,167**; international **AUD 6,238**. Worked example given in the schedule: an 8-hour nursing shift = **AUD 75.21**.

Four principles in that document matter directly to SQHN's argument:
- *"Fees are a contribution to the cost of activities associated with clinical education and training; they are not intended for full cost recovery."*
- *"Health services may not recoup costs associated with normal service delivery, or for the administration of clinical placements and other related costs."*
- *"It is acknowledged that in-kind contributions for clinical placements are made by both health and education stakeholders… Such contributions may be factored in the setting of fees."*
- Compliance with the Schedule *"is a condition of receiving the department's Training and Development – Professional-entry student placement subsidy."*

Source: [Fee schedule for clinical placement in public health services, health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/education-and-training/fee-schedule-for-clinical-placement-in-public-health-services) → document [standardised-schedule-fees-clinical-placements.docx](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-09/standardised-schedule-fees-clinical-placements.docx) (September 2024 issue, 2025 rates) — downloaded and read in full, 1 Aug 2026.

Note the Schedule **explicitly excludes** "Internships or graduate programs that are undertaken once the student has graduated" — an independent regulatory reason to keep the word "internship" out of SQHN's materials.

### 1.3 The supervision-burden evidence base

**EVALUATED / weak.** The Sax Institute Evidence Check (Bowles, Haines, Molloy, Maloney, Kent, Sevenhuysen & Tai, **July 2015**) found that *"no thorough investigation into the costs and benefits of clinical placements for health service organisations has been completed."* What it did surface:

- Supervisors provided an average of **4.3 hours per week directly supervising students** over a 10-week placement, decreasing as competence improved.
- Teaching costs in medical education not covered by universities averaged **$34,326 per student per year**, four times the university-funded amount. *(Currency and base year are not stated in the passage retrieved — treat as indicative only, not as a citable figure.)*
- Long-term (11-month) rural general practice placements were **"cost neutral or have a small positive financial" effect after a turning point of one to two months**, excluding non-clinical time.

Source: [The costs and benefits of providing undergraduate student clinical placements — Sax Institute Evidence Check](https://www.saxinstitute.org.au/wp-content/uploads/The-costs-and-benefits-of-providing-undergraduate-student-clinical-place....pdf) — accessed 1 Aug 2026.

**The operational implication is favourable to SQHN.** A 4.3 h/week supervision figure applies to a 10-week *hands-on* placement with assessment responsibility. A 40-hour observership with programme-side assessment and a tick-box host form is at the far low end of that distribution — and Victoria's rule that hosts may not recoup "normal service delivery" costs supports the argument that observation of existing activity is close to costless.

### 1.4 Observership as a *revenue* line for the host

**DOCUMENTED, and this is the finding that most changes the frame.** Hosts do not merely tolerate observers — internationally they charge them.

| Host | Fee | Period / scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Barts Health NHS Trust, London** | **GBP 1,600** total (£500 non-refundable deposit + £1,100 balance) | 4-week Clinical Observership; strictly observational, "hands-on clinical activity is not permitted at any stage"; requires an Honorary Contract which "Provides NHS indemnity cover"; deposit "contributes to costs incurred by the Trust to initiate pre-placement processes" |
| **Children's Hospital of Philadelphia** | **USD 750** administrative fee + background screening + possible tuition | 2–8 weeks; "participants cannot perform hands-on clinical care" |
| **Sir H. N. Reliance Foundation Hospital, Mumbai** | National: **INR 1,000 / 2,000 / 3,000 per week** (undergraduate-non-clinical / graduate / postgraduate). International: **USD 75 / 125 / 150 per week**. Plus 18% GST | 1–6, 6–12, 12+ weeks; "No hands-on patient care/contact/examination or research involvement is permitted" |

Sources: [Barts Health Academy Clinical Observership Programme](https://educationacademy.bartshealth.nhs.uk/our-teams/medical-education-clinical-observership-programme/); [CHOP International Observership Program](https://www.chop.edu/services/international-observership-program); [Reliance Foundation Hospital Observership](https://www.rfhospital.org/research-academics/education/observership) — all accessed 1 Aug 2026.

**Read this carefully.** At Reliance Foundation Hospital the *national* rate for a non-clinical observer is **INR 1,000/week ≈ 25 hours** — i.e. the going regional rate for exactly the product SQHN is buying is small but non-zero. It is not credible to assume a market where hosts universally give this away; it *is* credible to argue SQHN's package (indemnity carried, zero grading burden, toolkit licence, funded seats) is worth more to a Nigerian host than ₦-equivalent of INR 1,000/week.

## 2. What host sites get in return where placements are unpaid

### 2.1 Recruitment pipeline — the strongest evidenced benefit

**EVALUATED.** According to PubMed, Peiris, Lawton, Dunwoodie, Francis-Cracknell, Reubenson & Wells (2023), *Journal of Physiotherapy* 69(3):175–181, surveyed 45 Australian private practitioners hosting ~208 students per year (about one-third of all Australian physiotherapy private-practice placements) plus four focus groups. Finding: *"Participants reported that hosting placements helped to recruit graduates and assisted private practitioners in developing clinical and educator knowledge and skills."* Barriers: *"Cost (both time and financial) and difficulties securing a sufficient caseload."* Risk: *"potential for risk to business reputation and income when hosting a poorly performing student."* [DOI 10.1016/j.jphys.2023.05.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphys.2023.05.009)

The companion study, Peiris, Reubenson, Dunwoodie, Lawton, Francis-Cracknell & Wells (2021), *Journal of Physiotherapy* 68(1):61–68 (20 university clinical education managers, 95% response, data on 2,000 students): 44% of 2017 Australian physiotherapy graduates completed a 5-week private-practice placement; placements were *"perceived to be safe and beneficial for students, private practices and universities"*; *"The main risks identified… were related to the quality and consistency of the student's experience on placement and not risks to service or clients."* Main barriers: *"time costs… and perceived lost earning capacity."* [DOI 10.1016/j.jphys.2021.12.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphys.2021.12.007)

The Sax Institute Evidence Check reports one hospital finding that **40–50% of current staff were previous students** ([Sax Institute, July 2015](https://www.saxinstitute.org.au/wp-content/uploads/The-costs-and-benefits-of-providing-undergraduate-student-clinical-place....pdf)). **EVALUATED (single site).**

NHS Employers (publication dated 1 June 2026) states: *"Good placements help students learn safely, support future recruitment and strengthen workforce supply"* and *"Evidence shows that students who have a good placement experience will normally apply for a position within that organisation/specialty at the end of their training."* [NHS Employers — Student placements in the NHS](https://www.nhsemployers.org/publications/student-placements-nhs) — accessed 1 Aug 2026. **DESCRIBED** (no study cited on the page).

### 2.2 What hospital *leaders* say they get

**EVALUATED.** According to PubMed, DeBoer, Dockx, Lam, Shah, Young, Quesnel, Ng & Mori (2019), *Canadian Medical Education Journal* 10(1):e56–e67 (PMID 30949261) — 15 purposively sampled hospital leaders, face-to-face interviews, thematic analysis. Finding: *"opportunities stemming from the partnership were identified as research, current best practice, improved patient care, and career development"*; and *"Participants emphasized the value of hospital leaders prioritizing education."* Critically for a non-medical programme: *"The strength of the hospitals' relationship with the medical profession facilitated the partnership, however other health professions' partnerships were viewed less favourably."* [PubMed 30949261](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30949261/)

**Implication for SQHN:** a quality-and-safety observership is *not* a medical placement. This study is direct evidence that non-medical partnerships are ranked lower by hospital leadership — the recruitment pitch must therefore lead with something other than "you're helping educate."

### 2.3 What actually motivates the individual supervisor — and it is not money

This is the most exploitable finding in the whole dossier. **EVALUATED, four independent studies.**

- **Starr, Ferguson, Haley & Quirk (2003)**, *Academic Medicine* 78(8):820–5 — 35 experienced community preceptors, five structured focus groups. *"'Feeling intrinsic satisfaction' was the most common theme."* And: *"Although a group of participants were interested in 'receiving rewards for teaching,' rewards did not need to be financial compensation. For some, genuine recognition for their efforts by the medical school, particularly in the form of faculty development opportunities, constituted reward and recognition for teaching."* [DOI 10.1097/00001888-200308000-00017](https://doi.org/10.1097/00001888-200308000-00017)
- **Beck Dallaghan et al. (2017)**, *Academic Medicine* 92(8):1168–1174 — 51 preceptors, 12 institutions, multicentre qualitative. *"Time constraints and patient care demands were the most commonly cited deterrents to teaching… External rewards (e.g., recognition, continuing medical education credit) served as incentives."* [DOI 10.1097/ACM.0000000000001667](https://doi.org/10.1097/ACM.0000000000001667)
- **Ryan, Vanderbilt, Lewis & Madden (2013)**, *Medical Education Online* 18:20733 — comparison of active vs inactive preceptors. *"Preceptors showed similar demographic characteristics, valued intrinsic over extrinsic benefits, and appreciated Continuing Medical Education (CME)/Maintenance of Certification (MOC) opportunities as the highest extrinsic reward."* Active preceptors *"value recognition and faculty development opportunities (p<0.05)"*; inactive preceptors *"rated financial compensation as more important (p<0.05)."* [DOI 10.3402/meo.v18i0.20733](https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v18i0.20733)
- **Hjälmhult, Haaland & Litland (2012)**, *Nurse Education Today* 33(4):431–5 — grounded theory, 20 interviews + focus group. *"The preceptors were strongly concerned about invisibility and lack of recognition of the preceptor role… All stakeholders involved in clinical practice seem to contribute to making the preceptors' role invisible and thereby contribute to the lack of recognition, support and reward, which again seem to self-reinforce invisibility."* [DOI 10.1016/j.nedt.2012.09.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nedt.2012.09.004)

All four retrieved via PubMed.

**Synthesis, and it is directly actionable:** the four cheapest things SQHN can give are the four things the evidence says supervisors actually want — **(a) named, visible recognition; (b) CPD/CME credit; (c) faculty development (i.e. train the supervisor, free); (d) membership of an identified group of teachers.** Ryan's finding is the sharpest: the people who *decline* to precept are the ones who want money. Recruiting supervisors who are motivated by recognition is therefore also a selection filter for supervisors who will host for free.

## 3. Accreditation and regulatory credit as currency — investigated, and the answer is not the one hoped for

**Headline finding: no standards set examined awards a facility any credit for hosting learners. Several award credit for *having trained quality staff*. That is where the currency actually is.**

### 3.1 Nigeria — NHIA and the SafeCare/PharmAccess methodology

**DESCRIBED, direction confirmed, published rule NOT FOUND.**

PharmAccess/SafeCare state that under its partnership with NHIA, the Authority *"will rank healthcare facilities within her network based on their quality scores and compliance with critical criteria which will support performance-based contracting and payments"*, and that facilities failing to meet staffing, equipment and infrastructure requirements *"will not be accredited or re-accredited to provide services to health insurers."* Reported: 7,000+ facilities in the NHIA network; partnership commenced July 2021; a March 2024 strategy workshop developed *"its scoring algorithm and ranking methodology using key learnings from SafeCare's quality rating system"*; a July 2024 agreement granted NHIA access to SafeCare's Quality Evaluation System and Quality Platform. Training under that agreement was of **NHIA's own Standards and Quality Assurance Unit**, not of facilities.
Sources: [SafeCare — Nigeria's health insurance authority takes a big step forward on the quality improvement journey](https://www.safe-care.org/nigerias-health-insurance-authority-takes-a-big-step-forward-on-the-quality-improvement-journey/); [Mondaq mirror](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/healthcare/1700994/nigerias-health-insurance-authority-takes-a-big-step-forward-on-the-quality-improvement-journey) — accessed 1 Aug 2026.

**What could not be verified.** The NHIA Operational Guidelines 2023 PDF linked from [nhia.gov.ng/operational-guideline/](https://www.nhia.gov.ng/operational-guideline/) — namely `https://www.nhia.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NHIA-OPERATIONAL-GUIDELINES-20232.pdf` — returns **HTTP 404** (verified by direct HEAD request, 1 Aug 2026). The gazetted [NHIA Act 2022 PDF on nhia.gov.ng](https://www.nhia.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NHIA-Act-2022-Gazetted-Copy.pdf) returned **no machine-readable text** (image-only scan). The NHIA homepage exposes only two content links (`/download/nhia-book-3/` and `/operational-guideline/`). **No published NHIA facility-accreditation checklist, scoring rubric or criteria list was found.** Record as **BLOCKED / NOT FOUND**.

**Honest assessment.** NHIA quality-linked accreditation is real as *intent* and is being built on a methodology (SafeCare) whose standards do assess human resources and training. It is **not** currently a lever SQHN can point a host site at with a citation, because the operative document is not publicly retrievable. Booking any host-site value on "NHIA credit" today would be unevidenced.

### 3.2 SQHN's own hospital accreditation standards

**DOCUMENTED — and this is a lever SQHN directly controls.** From the *SQHN Accreditation Standards, Second Edition (new and revised standards)*, [sqhn.org PDF, uploaded August 2025](https://sqhn.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/SQHN-SECOND-EDITION-STANDARDS-_NEW-AND-REVISED-STANDARDS.pdf), accessed 1 Aug 2026:

| Standard | Wording | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **HR.4.5** | *"Competency assessments—such as structured orientation, direct-observation audits and refresher trainings—shall occur at least annually."* and *"Staff working in the hospital should have access to relevant, appropriate continuing education programmes to maintain their professional standards of practice."* | A QIPS-certified staff member is direct evidence for this standard |
| **QI 5.1 (Intent)** | *"Depending on the complexity of services rendered by the organisation, Management may appoint an individual to oversee the quality improvement programme of the hospital."* | Permissive ("may"), not mandatory — weaker than JCI/PHC equivalents |
| **PRE.6.5.1** | *"The organisation must state clearly if they are involved in research, teaching and development and must obtain consent from the intended patients."* | **The only teaching-related standard, and it is a disclosure-and-consent obligation, not a credit.** Hosting observers *creates* a compliance duty for the host, it does not earn points |
| LAB 7.3.9, LAB 7.10.7, RAD.8.4.3, MCH 1.5.9 | Role-specific training and competency requirements | Same logic as HR.4.5 |

SQHN also states it holds **ISQua reaccreditation** and sells its Hospital Accreditation Standards via Amazon; it runs a *Certificate in Introduction to Quality and Patient Safety (CIQPS)*, a Risk Management Certification Course, and a *Data-Driven Quality Improvement* course priced **₦40,000–₦50,000** (session dated 8 July 2026). Source: [sqhn.org](https://sqhn.org/) — accessed 1 Aug 2026. **DOCUMENTED.**

**The actionable move.** SQHN is both the programme sponsor and the accrediting body. It can *make* the currency: amend QI 5.1 at the next standards revision from "may appoint" to a graded criterion that recognises a formally qualified quality lead, and add an interpretive note that a QIPS-certified staff member satisfies HR.4.5 for the quality domain. That converts a training purchase into an accreditation asset. **This is the single highest-leverage, lowest-cost recommendation in this dossier.** It must be governed carefully — self-dealing between an accreditor and its own training programme is exactly the conflict ISQua EEA scrutinises — but a transparent, published, evidence-based criterion (qualified quality lead) is legitimate and is what JCI already does.

### 3.3 International standards — JCI, COHSASA, SafeCare, ISQua EEA

**JCI Accreditation Standards for Hospitals, 8th Edition, effective 1 January 2025** ([digitalassets.jointcommission.org sample](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/79e9f5d2cd444a1a9178ab3f9487889a?v=4d4ea7b7), accessed 1 Aug 2026) — **DOCUMENTED:**

**SafeCare** ([safe-care.org/who-we-are/safecare-standards/](https://www.safe-care.org/who-we-are/safecare-standards/), accessed 1 Aug 2026) — **DOCUMENTED:** *"SafeCare's standards are the only IEEA-accredited clinical standards tailor-made for resource-restricted settings"*; re-accredited by IEEA (site badge shows 2025–2029); standards *"cover a full range of clinical services and management functions."* The page does **not** confirm a Level 1–5 rating system or name human resources as a discrete service element — **NOT CONFIRMED on primary source**.

- Strategic Objective 4(i): *"Provide in-service training on quality improvement for workforce… and incorporate ethics and quality-related standards in the job description of health workers"* — targets of 10,000 health workers trained in QI by Q4/2018, 40,000 by Q4/2020, and 70% of training institutions delivering quality training by Q4/2021.

### 3.5 Pakistan — provincial healthcare commissions

**DOCUMENTED.** All three MSDS sets examined require a *designated quality individual* and *staff training* — and none credits hosting.

**Punjab Healthcare Commission, MSDS for Hospitals (2012)** ([lgh.org.pk PDF](https://lgh.org.pk/download/MSDS%20for%20Hospitals.pdf), accessed 1 Aug 2026):
- **Standard 16 (CQI-1):** *"There is a structured quality improvement and continuous monitoring programme in the organization."*
- **Indicator 95:** *"There is a designated individual for coordinating and implementing the quality improvement programme"* — with *"a job description for this individual that defines the requisite qualifications and the duties."* ← **explicit qualification hook**
- **Standard 18, Indicators 106–107:** defined sentinel events, intensively analysed.
- Standard 2 Ind 2 (laboratory) and Ind 9 (imaging): *"A system of Continuous Professional Development should be active."*
- Standard 14 Ind 89: medical-waste handling training.

**Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Healthcare Commission, MSDS Reference Manual Category II-A** ([hcc.kp.gov.pk PDF](https://hcc.kp.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/MSDS-Category-2-A-Hospitals.pdf)) and **Category II-B** ([PDF](https://hcc.kp.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/MSDS-for-Category-2-B-Hospitals.pdf)), accessed 1 Aug 2026:

### 3.6 Verdict on Section 3

**The hoped-for lever — "hosting observers earns accreditation credit" — does not exist in any of the six standards sets examined. In three of them (SQHN PRE.6.5.1, KP HRM-1, Sindh 4.5.14) hosting a learner *creates a compliance obligation for the host*, which is the opposite of a benefit.**

**The adjacent lever is real and stronger.** Every set examined requires a designated quality individual and staff training; two (PHC Indicator 95, JCI QPS.01.00) explicitly attach *qualification* to that individual. The sellable proposition is therefore not *"host us and gain accreditation points"* but:

> **"Send your quality lead on this programme and you will hold documented evidence for the human-resources and quality-management criteria in whichever standard you are assessed against — SQHN HR.4.5 and QI 5.1, PHC Indicator 95, KP Indicator 47, SHCC 4.5.16–4.5.18, JCI QPS.01.00 and SQE.01.07. Hosting observers is what you give in exchange for that seat."**

That reframes hosting from a favour into the *price of a funded seat*, and it is fully citable today.

### 4.3 The statutory rates — confirmed from legal secondary sources only

**DOCUMENTED (secondary).** The Industrial Training Fund Act (originally 1973 No. 37, CAP I9 LFN 2004):
- **s.6(1)** — *"every employer having twenty-five or more employees in his establishment"* shall contribute *"one per centum of the amount of his annual pay roll"* by 1 April (or prescribed date). **s.6(3)** allows the Minister to vary the rate by Federal Gazette order with Presidential approval. **s.6(4)** defines employees broadly, including temporary workers employed 3+ months annually.
- **s.7(1)** — *"The Council may make a refund of up to sixty per centum of the amount paid by an employer if the Council is satisfied that the training programme of the employer is adequate."*
Source: [The Jet Lawyer — Industrial Training Fund Act](https://djetlawyer.com/industrial-training-fund-act/) — accessed 1 Aug 2026.

**ITF (Amendment) Act 2011** — *"signed by the president on 3 June 2011 and gazetted on 22 June 2011"*:
- Contribution rate **unchanged at 1% of payroll**; payment due 1 April of the following year.
- **Threshold reduced from 25 employees to "a minimum of 5 employees or a turnover of ₦50 million and above per annum."**
- **Refund reduced from 60% to "50% of the amount paid by such employer subject to the training programme of the employer being in accordance with the Fund's reimbursement schemes."**
- Penalties: body corporate **₦500,000** first breach, **₦1,000,000** subsequent; officers **₦50,000 or two years' imprisonment** for a first breach, imprisonment without option of fine thereafter.
Source: [PwC Nigeria — *What you should know and do about the Industrial Training Fund (Amendment) Act 2011*, February 2012](https://www.pwc.com/ng/en/pdf/itf-amendment-february-2012.pdf) — accessed 1 Aug 2026.

**So the "25+ employees" figure in prior QIPS work is OUT OF DATE.** The current threshold is **5 employees or ₦50m annual turnover**, and the reimbursement ceiling is **50%, not 60%** — and per TR Form 1A that 50% is an outer cap subject to a per-training-area "Max % Claimable" and a discretionary "% Awarded".

### 4.4 The reimbursement process

**DOCUMENTED (secondary, corroborated by TR Form 1A).** Per [Taxaide, 21 March 2019](https://taxaide.com.ng/2019/03/21/do-you-know-that-you-can-be-reimbursed-with-up-to-50-of-your-itf-contributions/) — accessed 1 Aug 2026:
- Eligible: employers with ≥5 employees or ≥₦50m turnover who have paid the 1% contribution; may claim *"up to 50% of its contributions as reimbursements for trainings expenses spent in the previous year."*
- **Annual training plan must be submitted before 31 March.**
- **Course approval must be obtained from ITF in advance — 2 weeks ahead for local training, 3 weeks for international** (Form 4A).
- Claims filed on ITF TR Form 1 (summary) and TR Form 2 (areas of reimbursement award), plus approved Form 3A for training personnel, course approval, and cost details.
- **Claims deadline 30 June** — corroborated verbatim by the primary TR Form 1A.

### 4.5 Does the ITF levy survive the Nigeria Tax Act 2025?

**PARTIALLY VERIFIED.** The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 introduced a **4% Development Levy** on assessable profits of Nigerian companies (small companies exempt). PwC states it *"consolidates the Tertiary Education Tax (TET), Information Technology Levy (IT), the National Agency for Science and Engineering Infrastructure (NASENI) levy and the Police Trust Fund (PTF) levy"*, with proceeds earmarked education 50%, student loans 15%, technology development 8%, science and engineering infrastructure 8%, technology incubation 4%, defence and security 10%, cybersecurity 5%. Source: [PwC, *Nigerian Tax Reforms, 2025 — Tax Insight Series and Sectoral Analysis*](https://www.pwc.com/ng/en/assets/pdf/nigeria-tax-reform-insight-series-sectoral-analysis.pdf) — downloaded and text-searched, 1 Aug 2026. **The ITF levy is not named in any consolidation list found.** The reasonable inference is that the ITF 1% payroll contribution survives — but **this is inference from silence, not a found statement, and must be labelled as such.** A direct confirmation from FIRS or ITF was **NOT FOUND**.

### 4.6 Assessment of the ITF lever

**Verdict: keep it booked at zero for cohort 1.** Reasons, all evidenced above:
1. The reimbursement is to the **employer**, not to SQHN. It reduces the sponsoring hospital's net cost — a *sales* argument, never programme income.
2. It requires the sponsor to have filed an annual training plan **before 31 March** and obtained **advance ITF course approval 2–3 weeks before delivery**. For a January 2027 cohort, the sponsoring employer's plan window closes **31 March 2026 — already passed** for the 2026 training year, and **31 March 2027** for the 2027 year, i.e. *after* the cohort starts. **Any employer that has not already planned for it cannot claim for a January 2027 start.** This timing trap is the single most important practical finding on ITF and appears not to have been noted in prior work.
3. "Up to 50%" is an outer cap, further reduced by a per-area "Max % Claimable" and a discretionary "% Awarded".
4. The process for becoming an ITF-approved *training provider* was **NOT FOUND on any primary source**; what the sources describe is per-course approval sought by the **employer** via Form 4A — and Form 4A itself returns HTTP 404 on the ITF server.

**Recommended use:** publish an "ITF planning note" for Nigerian institutional sponsors telling them to include the programme in their **31 March 2027 training plan** in order to claim in the 2027 training year for a **cohort 2** intake, and to seek course approval 2 weeks before each module. Market it as buyer guidance, not as programme revenue.

### 5.1 The one government-published barter menu — Victoria, Australia

**DOCUMENTED, and this is the most directly reusable artefact found.** The Victorian schedule states as a governing principle: *"It is acknowledged that in-kind contributions for clinical placements are made by both health and education stakeholders and that these valuable contributions are of mutual benefit. Such contributions may be factored in the setting of fees. In-kind contributions are to be determined (by agreement) and on terms satisfactory to both partners."*

**Appendix 2, "Suggested options for in-kind arrangements"**, reproduced in full from [standardised-schedule-fees-clinical-placements.docx](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-09/standardised-schedule-fees-clinical-placements.docx) (accessed 1 Aug 2026):

**Options to enhance student supervision** — access to a clinical supervision course; access to adult-learning education including new or innovative teaching methods; development of and access to innovative models of clinical teaching and learning including simulation-centre modules and video-conferencing; access to supervisor support models across disciplines (facilitated groups or one-to-one); access to forums, workshops or other professional development on planning and management of placements; **provision of academic awards or prizes**.

**Options to enhance clinical staff expertise** — access to skills training and other professional development; **access to library, journal publications, online resources**; **subsidised post-graduate places in clinical areas through faculty scholarships**; provision of post-graduate units on-site; customised short courses, clinical practice updates and academic presentations on-site or by video-link/download; **access to affiliated associate or adjunct professorial posts or other joint appointments for service-based clinical experts, such as Adjunct Fellow or honorary positions**; opportunities for clinical staff to contribute to curriculum development or implementation, including during accreditation processes; access to revision opportunities for subjects such as anatomy and physiology.

**Options to enhance research opportunities and capacity** — development of Associate/Adjunct Professor roles within and across disciplines and multiple health services; support for research/clinical appointments enabling simultaneous clinical work and translational research, including development of clinical guidelines; access to research mentors; access to research seminars and/or research methodology training including statistical analysis, on-site or online; **assistance with writing grant applications and seeking funding opportunities**; allocation of research assistants to health services; presentation skills training; access to statistical software (e.g. SPSS); access to visiting professors; **joint research agreements**.

**Options to enhance operational aspects of service delivery** — provision of data and interpretation from Schools of Public Health; access to research and training opportunities from other areas of the provider such as Schools of Business and Education; access to statistical analysis; **opportunities to present at job expositions**.

**Cross-check against SQHN's existing offer table** (QIPS-Host-Site-Standard §7.5): SQHN already covers roughly two-thirds of this menu (funded seats, toolkit licence, benchmarking report, assessment report, faculty invitations, co-authorship, recognition mark, named contact). **Four items in the Victorian menu are absent from the SQHN table and are cheap to add:**
1. **Adjunct/honorary academic title** for the named supervisor — the item Starr (2003) and Ryan (2013) identify as the highest-value non-cash reward, and SQHN can confer it because it is a professional society.
2. **Access to library / journal / online resources** — near-zero marginal cost for a digital programme; disproportionately valued in LMIC facilities.
3. **Grant-application assistance** and **joint research agreements** — costly in time, but the single most credible route to converting a host into a long-term partner.
4. **Academic awards or prizes** for host-site supervisors — trivial cost, high visibility, and directly answers Hjälmhult's "invisibility" finding.

### 5.2 A documented no-money MoU

**DOCUMENTED.** Betsi Cadwaladr University Health Board and Coleg Cambria, **13 May 2026**: BCUHB provides clinical placements and employment opportunities; Coleg Cambria provides educational programmes, training facilities (a £14m building with simulated hospital wards and VR learning) and Welsh-language instruction; governance by a *"joint strategic steering group"*. **No money is exchanged** — it is *"formalised as a Memorandum of Understanding rather than a financial contract."* Source: [bcuhb.nhs.wales](https://bcuhb.nhs.wales/news/2026/landmark-partnership-to-further-develop-healthcare-staff-and-train-the-next-generation1/) — accessed 1 Aug 2026.

This is a usable structural precedent: **placements-for-training-capacity, governed by a joint steering group, no cash.** SQHN's host-site MoU can be drafted on the same logic.

## 6. Reciprocal and consortium models

**THIN — this lever is the weakest in the dossier.**

**DESCRIBED.** According to PubMed, Hodges & Kline (2005), *Journal for Nurses in Staff Development* 21(6):267–271, describe a **Clinical Placement Consortium** in Michigan: *"The process of planning the clinical rotations for nursing students can be challenging for both academia and the clinical service setting… The Clinical Placement Consortium (CPC) has served as a method to work toward a common goal of quality clinical experiences for all students."* Advantages and challenges presented from both views. [DOI 10.1097/00124645-200511000-00005](https://doi.org/10.1097/00124645-200511000-00005) — **this is a descriptive practice paper; it contains no cost data and no net-cost evaluation.**

**DOCUMENTED, adjacent sector.** Reciprocal exchange in higher education operates on a *balance* principle — institutions exchange places and reconcile imbalance over time rather than transferring cash. Live examples: [University of California UCEAP Reciprocity System](https://reciprocity.uceap.universityofcalifornia.edu/); [FSU Reciprocal Student Exchange Agreements](https://www.global.fsu.edu/global-footprint/global-partners/international-agreements/reciprocal-student-exchange-agreements); [University of South Carolina — Types of Agreements](https://sc.edu/about/offices_and_divisions/global_carolina/international_agreements/types_of_agreements/) — all accessed 1 Aug 2026.

**Assessment for SQHN.** A reciprocal model requires each party to *have learners to send*. SQHN's hosts are hospitals, not schools — they have no cohort to place. The reciprocity that is actually available to SQHN is **asymmetric barter**: the host gives observation days, SQHN gives seats. That is the model already in the Host Site Standard, and it is the right one. A true reciprocal consortium becomes possible only once ≥10 host sites exist and each is sending staff into the programme — at which point the "annual anonymised network benchmarking report" already in §7.5 becomes the consortium's shared asset. **Treat consortium reciprocity as a cohort-3 concept, not a cohort-1 lever.** No evidence was found that it reduces net cost.

## 7. What a host-site agreement contains on indemnity, liability and insurance for a non-clinical observer

This section carries the numbers that bear on the programme's open Q001.

### 7.1 The two governing models

| Model | Who carries the risk | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Host absorbs the observer into its own cover** | Host indemnity extends to the observer, usually via an honorary contract | **Barts Health**: *"All Clinical Observers must hold an Honorary Contract with Barts Health, which: Provides NHS indemnity cover"* and is *"subject to the same pre-employment checks as substantive staff."* **Newcastle upon Tyne Hospitals**: work experience placements do **not** require honorary contracts; *"Indemnity can be provided through CNST arrangements for non-college/university an individual, providing the learner is over 16."* |
| **B — Host explicitly excludes the observer** | Sending party or individual must carry cover | **London Health Sciences Centre / St Joseph's**: observers are explicitly **NOT** *"Covered under the organization's liability insurance"* and **NOT** *"Covered under the Workplace Safety and Insurance Board (WSIB)."* |

Sources: [Barts Health Academy](https://educationacademy.bartshealth.nhs.uk/our-teams/medical-education-clinical-observership-programme/); [Newcastle upon Tyne Hospitals — Work Experience and Honorary Contracts Policy, October 2019](https://www.newcastle-hospitals.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Work-Experience-and-Honorary-Contracts-Policy-October-2019.pdf); [LHSC/St Joseph's Observer (Medical and Non-Medical) Policy](https://www.sjhc.london.on.ca/media/4556/download) — all accessed 1 Aug 2026. **DOCUMENTED.**

**Both models exist in mature jurisdictions. SQHN cannot assume Model A in Nigeria, Ghana or Pakistan; it must be prepared to fund Model B.**

### 7.2 The GMC position — the sharpest statement of where cover stops

**DOCUMENTED.** [GMC, *Guidance on undergraduate clinical placements* — Annex](https://www.gmc-uk.org/education/standards-guidance-and-curricula/guidance/undergraduate-clinical-placements/guidance-on-undergraduate-clinical-placements/annex), accessed 1 Aug 2026:
- **England:** students providing care to NHS patients through NHS Trust placements are covered under the trust's **Clinical Negligence Scheme for Trusts**; GP placements under the Clinical Negligence Scheme for General Practice. **Critically: indemnity does not extend to *"non-NHS bodies delivering care or treatment, such as a charity."***
- **Scotland:** *"NHS Indemnity will apply to students engaged by Health Boards in Scotland, or who are working under the supervision of NHS Health Board employees"* or honorary contract holders.
- **Wales:** cover applies when the student is under *"[the] Health Body's management, supervision and control"*, and students must have *"a clear agreement which outlines the activities that will be undertaken by the student."*
- **Northern Ireland:** cover via HSC Trusts under Trust-employee supervision, but not for privately employed clinicians or independent GPs — *"All students are strongly recommended to have their own indemnity."*

**Read-across, and it is a warning.** SQHN is a professional society, i.e. a non-statutory body — precisely the category the GMC annex flags as falling outside institutional indemnity in England. In Nigeria there is no CNST equivalent. The default assumption must be that **no host-side scheme automatically covers a QIPS observer**, and that the programme must either buy cover or contract the risk expressly.

### 7.3 The published contractual figures — what a real deed demands

**DOCUMENTED, and these are the hard numbers.**

**Queensland Health Student Placement Deed, QLD-QH-SPD-XXX-22-01** ([careers.health.qld.gov.au PDF](https://www.careers.health.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/190378/Student-Placement-Deed-2022.pdf), accessed 1 Aug 2026):
- **Clause 16.1 — Public liability:** *"not less than $10 million per occurrence (Costs Exclusive)"*, covering students, vicarious liability for personnel/contractors, and abuse/molestation defence costs. **(AUD)**
- **Clause 16.1 — Professional indemnity / medical malpractice:** *"not less than $10,000,000 per claim and $20,000,000 in the annual aggregate"*, covering healthcare services, students, vicarious liability, and breach of confidentiality/privacy. **(AUD)**
- **Clause 16.1 — Personal accident:** for student injury/illness during placements, including vehicle and aircraft incidents.
- **Clause 16.1 — Cyber:** *"adequate to cover the maximum probable losses"*, Network Security and Privacy liability.
- **Clause 17 — Indemnity:** Education Provider indemnifies Queensland Health for losses from breaches, unlawful acts, negligence, IP infringement, and confidentiality breaches by the provider, its personnel **or students**.
- **Clause 7.5 — Status:** *"Students undertaking Placements: (a) are not staff, employees, agents, or contractors"* of Queensland Health, and may only perform placement-approved activities.

**UTS Work Placement Agreement** ([uts.edu.au PDF](https://www.uts.edu.au/globalassets/sites/default/files/work-placement-agreement.pdf), accessed 1 Aug 2026) — a lighter, non-clinical model:
- **Clause 3.4:** university holds personal accident insurance and public liability with *"limit of indemnity of not less than $10 million"* **(AUD)**.
- **Clause 4.4:** host organisation holds public liability with *"limit of indemnity of not less than $10 million"* **(AUD)** — i.e. **mutual**, not one-way.
- **Clause 3.5(a):** UTS indemnifies the organisation for claims from student injuries on premises, with carve-outs at 3.5(b)–(c) where the organisation caused or contributed.
- **Clause 4.3:** the organisation reciprocally indemnifies UTS **and the student**.
- **Clauses 2.4, 4.1(c), 5.2(a):** *"no party to this Agreement intends to create an employer/employee relationship"*; *"the Student is not an employee of the Organisation"*; the student *"is not an employee of UTS or the Organisation."*
- **No professional indemnity requirement on either party** — because the placement is non-clinical.

**This last point is decisive for SQHN.** The UTS non-clinical agreement carries **public liability only** and **no professional indemnity at all**. The Queensland clinical deed carries **AUD 10m/20m professional indemnity**. The difference between those two documents *is* the difference between a placement and an observership. If SQHN's Observer Scope of Practice annex is enforced — no patient contact, no records access, no unattended presence — then the correct product is **public liability / general liability with a personal accident rider, not medical malpractice cover**. That is a materially cheaper instrument, and the broker brief should say so explicitly.

**NSW Health Policy Directive PD2022_049, *Student Placements in NSW Health*, October 2022** ([health.nsw.gov.au PDF](https://www1.health.nsw.gov.au/pds/ActivePDSDocuments/PD2022_049.pdf), accessed 1 Aug 2026): *"An executed (signed) Student Placement Agreement is required between an Education Institution and a Public Health Organisation"*, and the SPA *"details various other terms including, but not limited to, governance arrangement, operational arrangements… indemnity insurance and dispute resolution."* The directive itself carries **no amounts and no fees**, and **does not differentiate observation-only placements** from other placements — every placement needs the same executed agreement. See also the NSW Health Student Placement Agreement information bulletin [IB2021_025](https://www1.health.nsw.gov.au/pds/ActivePDSDocuments/IB2021_025.pdf).

### 7.4 Standard non-financial clauses observers are held to

**DOCUMENTED.** Consistent across the LHSC, Newcastle, Miami and Reliance policies:
- **Absolute prohibition on patient care.** LHSC: observers may not undertake *"Taking a medical history, Conducting physical examinations, Diagnosing or treating patient's condition, Ordering, preparing or administering drugs, Documenting on patients' health records… Performing or assisting in surgical procedures… Obtaining consent, Interacting directly with patient/substitute decision maker (SDM), and Providing health care advice."* Newcastle: *"An individual granted observer status is not authorised to participate in patient care or treatment."* Miami: *"Participant shall in no way be permitted to actively participate in patient care or contact, examination, research or other work during his/her observership."*
- **Continuous supervision.** LHSC: *"The Observer is under the responsibility of a LHSC staff or affiliate at all times."*
- **Confidentiality.** LHSC requires observers to *"hold in confidence all information about patients, residents, clients, and their families, staff and affiliates, as well as the confidential business information."* Newcastle requires signature of a named Confidentiality of Medical Employment Records / E-mail and Internet Usage Statement.
- **Health screening.** LHSC requires a *"Medical and Non-Medical Observers – Self-Screening Health Evaluation"*; Newcastle refers declared conditions to Occupational Health and states *"The placement will not go ahead unless there is clearance"*; Barts requires OH clearance and immunisation checks, with offers withdrawn on failure.
- **Three-signature authorisation.** LHSC requires the Observer, a Sponsor (credentialed staff) **and** the Department/Division/Site Chief or Programme Director.
- **Release and hold-harmless from the individual.** University of Miami: *"I hereby agree to release, indemnify and hold harmless the University of Miami… from and against any and all losses, expenses, claims, actions, liabilities and judgments… which I… may sustain or suffer."* ([UM International Observership Program Policy and Procedure Manual](https://isss.miami.edu/scholars/um-host-departments/international-observers-miller-school-of-medicine/92-iop-policy-and-procedure-manual-medical-school.pdf))
- **Health insurance on the individual, not the institution.** Miami: for observerships longer than two weeks, *"proof of medical insurance adequate to cover the Participant's expenses in the event the Participant becomes ill or is injured… including expenses of repatriation should it become necessary."*

**Two cheap risk transfers available to SQHN immediately, both precedented:** (a) an individual participant **release and hold-harmless** on the Miami model; (b) a requirement that each participant hold **personal medical/travel insurance** for the observership block, as a condition of enrolment — which is consistent with the existing rule that travel and accommodation are met by the participant or sponsor.

### 7.5 Nigeria, Ghana, Pakistan

**NOT FOUND.** No published host-site agreement, observer policy, or indemnity template from a Nigerian, Ghanaian or Pakistani health facility was located, and no published premium data for observer cover in those markets exists. Sindh's standard **4.5.14** creates a *supervision* obligation but says nothing about insurance. See NOT FOUND register N9–N10.

## Assessment — which levers are real, which are speculative

### Real, evidenced, usable now

1. **"Trained quality staff" as accreditation currency.** Every standards set examined requires a designated quality individual and staff training; PHC Indicator 95 and JCI QPS.01.00 attach *qualification* to that role. SQHN can cite chapter and verse in all three target markets **today**. This is the strongest lever found, and it is a *seat-sales* lever, not a hosting lever.

2. **SQHN's dual role as accreditor and sponsor.** SQHN owns the standard against which Nigerian hosts are assessed. Tightening QI 5.1 from "may appoint" to a graded, published criterion recognising a formally qualified quality lead creates the credit that does not currently exist anywhere. Highest leverage, near-zero cost — but must be published transparently and justified on evidence, or it reads as self-dealing and imperils SQHN's own ISQua standing.

3. **Recognition and faculty development as supervisor payment.** Four independent peer-reviewed studies say the same thing: recognition, CPD/CME credit and faculty development substitute for money, and preceptors who want money are the ones who decline to teach anyway. Adjunct/honorary title, named recognition, prizes, and a free supervisor development course are the cheapest real currency SQHN has.

4. **Recruitment pipeline.** Evidenced (Peiris 2023; Sax Institute 40–50% single-site figure). Real, but note DeBoer 2019: hospital leaders rank *non-medical* partnerships lower. Do not lead with it.

5. **Observership has a market price and hosts charge it.** Barts £1,600/4 weeks, CHOP USD 750, Reliance INR 1,000–3,000/week. SQHN's "no fee is paid and none is expected" default is a *negotiating position*, not a market fact. Budget a contingency for sites that ask.

6. **Indemnity is a real, priced obligation — but the right instrument is cheaper than assumed.** The UTS non-clinical agreement carries **public liability only, no professional indemnity**; the Queensland clinical deed carries AUD 10m/20m malpractice cover. A properly scoped non-clinical observership sits in the first category. **This should materially move the answer to Q001 and therefore the USD 75/learner trigger in F026.**

### Speculative, or to be booked at zero

7. **"Hosting observers earns accreditation credit."** **Not found in any of six standards sets.** In SQHN's own PRE.6.5.1, in KP's HRM-1, and in Sindh's 4.5.14 it is the reverse — hosting creates a duty. **Remove this from the recruitment argument.**

8. **NHIA quality-linked accreditation as a host-site benefit.** Directionally real, publicly unverifiable: the NHIA Operational Guidelines PDF is 404 and the Act PDF is an unreadable scan. Do not cite it to hosts until a retrievable document exists.

9. **ITF reimbursement.** Rules confirmed (1% payroll; ≥5 employees or ≥₦50m turnover; up to 50% refund; claims by 30 June) but the primary website is unreachable, "up to 50%" is capped and discretionary, the money goes to the *employer* not to SQHN, and — **the finding that decides it** — the 31 March training-plan deadline and 2–3 week advance course-approval requirement mean **no employer can claim for a January 2027 cohort unless it planned for it by 31 March 2026, which has passed.** Keep at zero for cohort 1; publish it as buyer guidance for cohort 2.

10. **Reciprocal consortium.** One 2005 descriptive paper, no cost evaluation, and structurally inapplicable because SQHN's hosts have no learners to send. Cohort-3 concept at the earliest.

11. **JCI Academic Medical Center status.** Attaches to medical education and human-subjects research; eligibility document 404. A 40-hour non-clinical observership would not qualify a facility. Do not imply otherwise.

### The three specific changes this evidence supports

- **Rewrite the host-site pitch.** Lead with the *seat*, not the *host role*: "your quality lead's certification is evidence for [named standard clause]; hosting is what you give for a funded seat." That is citable in all three markets today; "hosting earns you accreditation credit" is not citable anywhere.
- **Reissue the broker brief with the correct product.** Specify **public liability / general liability plus personal accident**, expressly *not* medical malpractice, citing the UTS non-clinical precedent and the Observer Scope of Practice annex; ask the broker to price both so the difference is visible. Add a participant release-and-hold-harmless (Miami model) and a mandatory participant personal medical insurance condition.
- **Add four items to the §7.5 offer table**, all drawn from the Victorian government menu and all near-zero cost: adjunct/honorary title for the named supervisor; library/journal/online resource access; grant-writing support and joint research agreements; an annual host-supervisor award.

# QIPS data-governance and legal instruction pack

Status: working external-instruction draft; not legal advice or canon

Date: 2026-07-28

## Decisions this pack must unlock

- Q009: the retention schedule and named data-protection contact.
- The operational meaning of the canon statement that SQHN and country partners are data controllers.
- The lawful and contractually documented route for cross-border programme data.
- The minimum participant, partner, platform and host-site terms required before launch.

## Current evidence position

Official sources confirm an enacted data-protection statute in Nigeria and regulator-stated organisational duties in Ghana. Pakistan's official sources reviewed to date confirm PECA 2016, describe a May 2023 personal-data text as draft legislation, and show one 2023 private-member bill as lapsed. A 2026 parliamentary answer describes later proposed personal-data legislation but is not, by itself, evidence of assent, commencement or operative text.

The programme must therefore obtain current country-specific legal confirmation before treating a Pakistan personal-data instrument as law in force. The evidence register at `workstreams/W17/working/run-001-evidence-register.md` records source scope and limitations.

## Interim data boundary

Until the W17 decision pack is approved:

- Base44 may hold control-room workflow records, evidence metadata, document links, status, comments and committee decisions.
- Base44 must not hold patient information, clinical case data, passport or government-ID copies, payment-card data, signed legal instruments, medical information, safeguarding files, insurance claims or raw research datasets.
- GitHub holds canon, metadata, text-first working papers and decision history; it is not a participant-record system.
- Google Drive holds committee documents under named-user access; sensitive folders require a separate access review and must not be linked publicly.
- Every proposed platform or integration must be entered in the processing inventory before participant data is collected.

## Processing inventory to complete

For every data flow record:

1. business purpose and programme owner;
2. data subjects and data fields;
3. source and collection notice;
4. controller, any joint controller and each processor/subprocessor;
5. lawful basis or other legal justification by jurisdiction;
6. system, storage region, access roles and authentication method;
7. countries from which the data is accessed or to which it is transferred;
8. transfer instrument or local legal basis;
9. record class, retention trigger and disposal method;
10. security, backup, audit and incident controls;
11. rights-request route and accountable contact;
12. evidence of approval and last review date.

## Record-class retention schedule for counsel review

The programme should not adopt one universal number. Counsel and the accountable record owner should approve a schedule for each class after mapping law, contract, limitation periods, accreditation needs, finance rules and research consent.

| Record class | Working trigger | Decision questions before a period is assigned |
|---|---|---|
| Enquiries and incomplete applications | Last meaningful interaction or application-cycle close | Marketing consent, suppression list, conversion analysis and deletion request handling |
| Unsuccessful applications | Final decision / appeal close | Fairness review, complaint defence and future reapplication |
| Participant master record | Completion, withdrawal or termination | Credential verification, alumni service, complaints and limitation periods |
| Assessment evidence and feedback | Final award / appeal close | Academic integrity, appeal window, external verification and credential audit |
| Attendance and engagement records | Programme completion | Award eligibility, learner support and aggregate evaluation |
| Observership and host-site records | Placement close | Safety, supervision, host agreement, incident defence and insurance requirements |
| Financial and tax records | Transaction or financial-year close | Statutory finance/tax retention in the contracting jurisdiction |
| Consent and privacy notices | Supersession or withdrawal plus linked record life | Proof of notice, consent version and lawful processing |
| Complaints, safeguarding and incident files | Case closure | Limitation periods, regulator/insurer notice, safeguarding duties and restricted access |
| Insurance and claim files | Policy or claim closure | Policy wording, insurer instruction and legal hold |
| Research and improvement datasets | Project close or consent-defined trigger | Ethics approval, de-identification standard, withdrawal terms and publication plan |
| System security and audit logs | Event date | Threat detection, investigation need, platform capability and proportionality |

No period is approved merely because it appears common in another organisation. A legal hold, complaint, investigation or claim suspends routine deletion for the affected records.

## Data-protection accountability options

### Option A — one programme privacy lead with country advisers

SQHN names one accountable programme privacy lead, each country partner names a local compliance contact, and counsel confirms whether any statutory DPO or supervisor appointment is required. This gives participants one front door while preserving local expertise.

### Option B — formal DPO function with country deputies

An internal or outsourced DPO function supervises the full programme, supported by country deputies and a documented independence/escalation route. This is stronger where scale, sensitive data or monitoring justifies the cost and formal role.

### Option C — distributed partner contacts only

Each controller handles its own notices, rights requests and incidents. This is not recommended for cohort 1 because participants would face fragmented accountability and cross-partner events could be mishandled.

Working recommendation: develop Option A as the minimum operating design, subject to country counsel confirming whether Option B or a specific statutory appointment is required. Record the person's name, employer, authority, mailbox, deputy and escalation route before launch.

## Joint-controller and processor questions for counsel

1. Does the current canon allocation make each named partner a joint controller for every activity, or should roles vary by processing purpose?
2. What joint-controller arrangement, public summary and allocation of rights-request duties are required?
3. Which platforms, faculty, coaches, host sites and service providers are processors, independent controllers or authorised persons?
4. What data-processing clauses and audit/subprocessor controls must appear in each agreement?
5. What transfer mechanisms and risk assessments are required between Nigeria, Ghana, Pakistan and any hosting country?
6. Which entity receives and coordinates access, correction, deletion, objection, portability and complaint requests?
7. Which incidents must be notified, to whom, and within what time in each jurisdiction?

## Pakistan status questions for local counsel

- Identify every personal-data instrument currently enacted and in force, with Gazette citation, assent and commencement date.
- Confirm the present legal effect of the 2023 draft and any 2026 government instrument.
- Explain applicable cross-border, localisation, registration, security, notice, consent, children's-data and breach duties.
- Explain PECA provisions relevant to programme systems without treating PECA as a complete data-protection code.

## Participant and partner legal-instrument checklist

Before launch, qualified counsel should settle:

- participant terms, programme scope, fees/refunds, conduct, academic integrity, complaints and appeals;
- observership limits, no-clinical-practice acknowledgement, host rules, safeguarding and incident reporting;
- privacy notice, research/marketing choices and separate consent where required;
- partner roles, decision rights, revenue, IP, confidentiality, data responsibilities and exit;
- host-site agreement, supervisor responsibilities, insurance, indemnity and access to records;
- faculty/coach appointment, credentials, conflicts, confidentiality, IP and consent to publicity;
- processor agreements and approved subprocessors;
- security, business continuity, breach coordination and deletion/return on exit.

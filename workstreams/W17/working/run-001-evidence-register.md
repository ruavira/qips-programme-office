# W17 run 001 — primary-source evidence register

Accessed: 2026-07-28

This register records what the cited source supports and, equally importantly, what it does not yet establish. It is working evidence, not legal advice or canon.

## E-W17-001 — Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023

- Publisher: Nigeria Data Protection Commission
- Source: https://ndpc.gov.ng/download/nigeria-data-protection-act-2023
- Status: verified official source
- Supports: Nigeria has an enacted national data-protection statute; the regulator states that it applies to organisations operating in Nigeria and certain processing of people in Nigeria. The regulator identifies sections 41–43 as governing transfers to foreign countries.
- Does not establish: the programme's lawful basis, controller/processor allocation, retention schedule, transfer instrument or compliance classification.
- Programme consequence: map every Nigerian data flow and obtain qualified review before participant data is transferred across borders.

## E-W17-002 — Ghana requirements for organisations

- Publisher: Data Protection Commission, Ghana
- Source: https://dataprotection.org.gh/for-organisations/
- Status: verified official source
- Supports: the Commission says organisations processing personal data must register, renew registration every two years, appoint and train a data-protection supervisor, publish an organisational privacy policy and apply appropriate security measures. It points to section 58 for designation of a compliance person.
- Does not establish: which QIPS entity is the Ghana controller, the programme's retention periods, or whether a particular processing operation is exempt.
- Programme consequence: no Ghana-facing participant-data workflow should launch until controller identity, registration responsibility and the named compliance role are assigned.

## E-W17-003 — Pakistan Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, 2016

- Publisher: Pakistan Code, Ministry of Law and Justice
- Source: https://pakistancode.gov.pk/english/UY2FqaJw1-apaUY2Fqa-apaUY2Jvbp8%253D-sg-jjjjjjjjjjjjj
- Status: verified official source
- Supports: PECA 2016 is presented in the official federal-law database as enacted legislation.
- Does not establish: a comprehensive participant-data governance regime equivalent to Nigeria's or Ghana's data-protection statute.
- Programme consequence: PECA must be considered in the Pakistan risk assessment, but it must not be used as a substitute for confirming the current status and effect of dedicated personal-data legislation.

## E-W17-005 — Pakistan Senate status of the 2023 bill

- Publisher: Senate of Pakistan
- Source: https://www.senate.gov.pk/en/pbs.php?catid=1&cattitle=Bills&leftcatid=278&subcatid=276
- Status: verified official source
- Supports: the Senate register says the private-member Personal Data Protection Bill, 2023 lapsed and was not passed.
- Does not establish: the status of a separate government bill or any instrument after that parliamentary item.
- Programme consequence: the lapsed bill cannot be relied on as current law.

## E-W17-006 — Pakistan National Assembly answer referring to a 2026 instrument

- Publisher: National Assembly of Pakistan
- Source: https://www.na.gov.pk/uploads/documents/questions/6a05b6b9ac21f_317.pdf
- Status: verified official source, legal status unresolved
- Supports: a 2026 parliamentary answer says MoITT had finalised comprehensive personal-data legislation and describes proposed rights and cross-border controls.
- Does not establish: assent, commencement, Gazette citation or operative text. The answer alone is insufficient to treat the instrument as law in force.
- Programme consequence: obtain the Gazette or enacted text, commencement evidence and local-counsel confirmation before relying on any 2026 provisions.

## Current findings and unresolved decisions

1. Q009 remains open. The sources support a purpose-limited, documented retention approach, but they do not supply one universal retention period for all QIPS records.
2. The programme needs a record-class schedule rather than a single number: enquiries, unsuccessful applications, participant records, assessment evidence, financial records, incident files and research datasets have different purposes and legal constraints.
3. A named responsible privacy role is required before collecting participant data. The final legal title and organisational home must follow the selected contracting entity and jurisdictional advice.
4. Until the legal review is complete, the safest operational control is data minimisation: Base44 holds programme workflow metadata only, while participant and sensitive information remains outside the current control-room layer.

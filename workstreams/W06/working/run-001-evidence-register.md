# W06 run 001 — evidence register

Accessed: 2026-07-29

This evidence supports a restricted coaching operating design. It does not appoint a coach,
approve a ratio or claim an external coaching credential.

## E-W06-001 — IHI Improvement Coach Professional Development Program

- Publisher: Institute for Healthcare Improvement
- Source: https://www.ihi.org/learn/courses/improvement-coach-professional-development-program
- Type: official programme benchmark
- Supports: improvement coaching joins improvement science, facilitation and the human side of
  change; it expects prior improvement knowledge, practice with a real team and continuing
  application between live sessions.
- Does not establish: a QIPS coach-to-participant ratio, appointment standard or equivalence to an
  IHI credential.

## E-W06-002 — IHI Model for Improvement

- Publisher: Institute for Healthcare Improvement
- Source: https://www.ihi.org/library/model-for-improvement
- Type: official method benchmark
- Supports: aims, measures, change ideas and PDSA testing form a coherent improvement method that
  coaches must be able to interrogate without doing the participant's work.
- Does not establish: the final QIPS capstone method or assessment authority.

## E-W06-003 — Quality Coach Development Programme leader guide

- Publisher: Q Community / Quality Coach Design Group hosted by the Health Foundation
- Source: https://q.health.org.uk/assets/resources/QCDP-PLeader-FINAL.pdf?v=1725024172
- Type: official programme guide
- Supports: experienced improvement practitioners serve as trainers, faculty and mentors; mentors
  coach practice, monitor self-directed work and act as critical friends, with indicative recurring
  time commitments.
- Does not establish: a universal ratio or a QIPS staffing number.

## E-W06-004 — NHS England improvement capacity guidance

- Publisher: NHS England
- Source: https://www.england.nhs.uk/nhsimpact/about-nhs-impact/how-to-build-capacity-and-capability-for-improvement/
- Type: official system guidance
- Supports: teams applying improvement need close, skilled support and coaching as part of a wider
  capability system.
- Does not establish: that coaching alone produces results or replaces leadership and sponsorship.

## E-W06-005 — ICF Code of Ethics

- Publisher: International Coaching Federation
- Source: https://coachingfederation.org/resource/icf-code-of-ethics/
- Type: official professional benchmark
- Supports: clarity of role, confidentiality, conflict management, fair treatment, accountability
  and professional boundaries are recognised coaching safeguards.
- Does not establish: that QIPS coaches are ICF members or credential-holders.

## E-W06-006 — ICF coaching-supervision competencies

- Publisher: International Coaching Federation
- Source: https://coachingfederation.org/app/uploads/2024/10/icf-cs-coachingsupervisioncompetencies-2024.09.10.pdf
- Type: official professional benchmark
- Supports: supervision is a reflective process that develops professional and ethical capacity;
  it should distinguish coaching, supervision, performance assessment and therapy.
- Does not establish: a mandatory QIPS supervisor credential.

## Evidence conclusion

The sources converge on applied practice, clear boundaries, recurring reflective support and
competence demonstrated in work rather than reputation alone. No reviewed primary source supplies
a universally defensible ratio. QIPS should capacity-test ratios against real artefact volume and
coach availability instead of publishing an invented number.

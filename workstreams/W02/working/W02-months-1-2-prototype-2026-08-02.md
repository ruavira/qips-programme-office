## Month 1 — Problem definition and improvement opportunity (A01)

### Performance outcome

The participant can state a quality or safety problem in their own service in terms that are
specific, bounded, evidenced and within their lawful influence — **and can say what they do not
yet know.**

That last clause is the one that matters. The admissions calibration found a vague improvement
challenge to be the most common weakness, on the heaviest-weighted criterion. A participant who
arrives with a confident but unfounded problem statement is in a worse position than one who
arrives with an honest question, because the confident one will not revise.

### Learning map

| Sequence | What the participant does | Time | Serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reads two contrasting problem statements — one well formed, one not — and says which is which and why | 45 min | The distinction the artefact is judged on |
| 2 | Works through scope, setting, affected population and lawful access as four separate questions | 90 min | Each is a named failure mode in the rubric |
| 3 | Tests a draft statement against a "so what would change?" prompt | 60 min | Bounded scope |
| 4 | Separates what is known from what is believed, and sources the known | 90 min | Evidence discipline |
| 5 | Knowledge check: eight items, formative, unlimited attempts | 15 min | Retrieval, not gatekeeping |
| **eLearning subtotal** | | **~6.7 h** | |
| Live session | See below | 3 h | |
| Artefact production | Drafting A01 against the brief | 5 h | |
| Coaching call | Small group, eight participants | 1 h | |
| **Month 1 total** | | **~15.7 h** | |

### Live session plan — Saturday 9 January 2027, 3 hours

This is the first time the cohort meets. Two things have to happen: the method has to start, and
the room has to become a room.

| | Segment | Minutes | Notes for faculty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening: what this year asks of you, and what it does not | 15 | State the time commitment honestly, including that weeks are not uniform. Do not oversell. |
| 2 | Participants state their problem in ninety seconds each, in breakouts of eight | 40 | Same grouping as the coaching groups, so the coaching relationship starts here |
| 3 | Faculty works one volunteered problem in front of the room, badly then well | 35 | The single highest-value segment. Show the revision, not the answer. |
| 4 | Break | 15 | |
| 5 | The four questions applied to your own problem, working alone with faculty circulating | 45 | Silent working time is deliberate |
| 6 | Two participants share a revised statement; the room critiques against the rubric | 25 | Introduces the rubric as a tool rather than a verdict |
| 7 | What good looks like in A01, and what to do this month | 15 | |

**Facilitation note.** Segment 3 requires a faculty member willing to be publicly wrong on
purpose. That is a casting decision, not a content decision, and it belongs in the W05 slot brief.

### Artefact brief — A01

**Submit by Tuesday 26 January 2027.** Roughly 1,200 words plus any attachments.

1. A problem statement with scope, setting and affected population.
2. What is currently known, and where that knowledge comes from.
3. What is not known, **stated as questions rather than as gaps**.
4. The team and setting you have lawful access to, and the basis of that access.
5. What is realistically changeable in twelve months, and what is not.

**No patient-identifiable information.** Stated on the form and repeated here.

### Draft rubric — A01

Four levels, 0 to 3. **DRAFT.** Final rubrics are not authorised.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Specificity | A topic, not a problem | A problem, unbounded | Bounded in scope | Bounded in scope, setting and population |
| Evidence | Assertion only | Some evidence, unsourced | Sourced evidence | Sourced, with its limits stated |
| Honest uncertainty | None expressed | Gaps listed | Gaps stated as answerable questions | Questions ordered by what would change the plan |
| Lawful access | Not addressed | Asserted | Named setting and team | Named, with the basis of access stated |
| Feasibility | No boundary drawn | Boundary asserted | Changeable and unchangeable distinguished | Distinction justified against the participant's actual influence |

**Not a pass mark.** No threshold is set here, and none may be derived from this table.

### Accessibility equivalents

Built alongside, not retrofitted. Sub-Saharan African online CPD evidence puts slow internet at
55% and limited access at 41% as reported barriers, **above cost** — so this is a completion risk,
not a courtesy.

- Every eLearning sequence available as a printable document with the same worked examples.
- Live session recorded; a 20-minute audio-only summary published within 48 hours.
- All video captioned; no meaning carried by colour alone.
- The artefact accepted as a document, a structured form, or a voice recording with a transcript.
- Total download for month 1 under 25 MB in the low-bandwidth path.

## Month 2 — Process map and current-state analysis (A02)

### Performance outcome

The participant can map how the work **actually** happens — not how policy says it happens — and
can identify where the process fails the people it serves.

### Learning map

| Sequence | What the participant does | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Compares a policy flowchart with an observed process for the same task | 60 min |
| 2 | Learns and practises one mapping notation, deliberately only one | 90 min |
| 3 | Plans how to map *with* the people who do the work rather than about them | 75 min |
| 4 | Identifies failure points and distinguishes them from irritations | 90 min |
| 5 | Knowledge check | 15 min |
| **eLearning subtotal** | | **~6.7 h** |

### Live session plan — Saturday 6 February 2027, 3 hours

| | Segment | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What month 1 revealed: patterns across the cohort's A01 submissions | 20 |
| 2 | Mapping demonstration on a real, messy process | 40 |
| 3 | Participants map a supplied scenario in pairs | 45 |
| 4 | Break | 15 |
| 5 | "Where does this process fail someone?" — failure-point identification against the same scenario | 40 |
| 6 | Planning your own mapping conversation: who, when, how you ask | 20 |
| 7 | A02 brief and month 2 close | 10 |

Segment 1 requires faculty to have read the cohort's month 1 artefacts. That is a real preparation
cost and it is in the timing log below.

### Artefact brief — A02

**Submit by Tuesday 23 February 2027.**

1. A process map of the current state, produced **with** people who do the work.
2. At least three named failure points, with the evidence for each.
3. A short account of what surprised you.
4. Which failure point the improvement will target, and why that one.

### Draft rubric — A02

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fidelity to actual practice | Policy restated | Some observation | Observed and confirmed with staff | Observed, confirmed, and divergences from policy named |
| Failure points | None identified | Identified without evidence | Three with evidence | Three with evidence, distinguished from irritations |
| Participation | Mapped alone | Staff consulted after the fact | Staff involved in mapping | Staff involved, and their disagreements recorded |
| Surprise | None recorded | Recorded generically | Specific and honest | Specific, honest, and changes the target |
| Target selection | Not stated | Stated | Stated with reasoning | Reasoning connects to A01's problem statement |

## Timing log — Item 1 condition 3

Condition 3 requires every prototype to record participant time, faculty preparation time, coach
review time, accessibility findings and unresolved assumptions.

### Participant time

Recomputed by `engine/curriculum.py --time-report` on every push, and checked against F019:

```
per month                 15.7 h
x 12 months              188.0 h
observership (F006)       40.0 h
TOTAL                    228.0 h    published (F019) 228.0 h    difference +0.0
ordinary week             3.92 h    published 4
```

**A finding for the committee.** The first version of this time model was built forwards from
plausible-looking figures and produced **178 hours — fifty short of the approved 228**, with an
ordinary week of 1.88 against a published 4. The validator caught it. The model is now derived
backwards from F019, which is what F019 says it should be.

**A second finding, and this one needs a ruling.** F019's phrase *"about 4 hours a week in an
ordinary week"* admits two readings. On the reading used here — a week in an ordinary month —
the figure is **3.92 hours**. On the narrower reading — a week containing neither the live session
nor the coaching call — it is **2.92 hours**, and the live-session week runs to about **6.9**.
Both are defensible from the wording. **On either reading, "about 4 hours a week" is not a claim
about every week**, and W14 must not present it as one.

### Faculty preparation time — estimated, untested

| | Month 1 | Month 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Session design review | 2 h | 1 h |
| Rehearsal, including the deliberate-failure segment | 2 h | 1 h |
| **Reading the cohort's prior artefacts** | — | 4 h |
| Delivery | 3 h | 3 h |
| **Total per faculty member** | **7 h** | **9 h** |

Month 2 is the expensive one, and it is expensive because segment 1 requires faculty to have read
forty artefacts. That cost repeats every month from February onward and **has not been priced into
F029**. It is the strongest argument found so far for the coverage-slot model holding a genuine
backup — a faculty member who cannot read forty artefacts in a given month cannot simply be
replaced on the day.

### Coach review time — estimated, untested

At a coaching group of eight: 20 minutes per artefact read before the call, plus the 1-hour call,
plus 15 minutes of notes. **Roughly 4 hours per group per month.** At the F025 target of 40
participants that is five groups and **20 coach-hours a month**.

UA3 records the assumption that eight artefacts can be reviewed meaningfully in one call. It is
untested and it is load-bearing.

### Accessibility findings

- The mapping notation in month 2 is inherently visual. A screen-reader equivalent needs a
  structured-text notation designed deliberately, not a described image. **Unsolved.**
- The 25 MB low-bandwidth ceiling holds for month 1. Month 2's mapping demonstration is
  video-heavy and does not yet fit. **Unsolved.**
- Voice-recorded artefact submission needs a transcription route. Unpriced.

### Unresolved assumptions

Held in the spine as UA1 to UA5 with owners. In short: four hours in an ordinary week is untested;
lawful process access by month 2 is untested and would stall the whole spine if it fails; eight
artefacts per coaching call is untested; low-bandwidth equivalents may preserve content without
preserving learning; and month 3 measurement may not yield enough points for a month 9 run chart.

## Country adaptation — Item 1 condition 5 as amended

Built for Nigeria. Adaptation demonstrated, not built, exactly as the amended condition requires.

Four adaptation points are named in the spine: worked examples, regulatory references, observership
logistics, and live-session timing. What does **not** adapt is the twelve-artefact spine, any
performance outcome, the monthly cycle, or the assessment of the participant's own analysis —
because the participant applies the method to their own facility, which makes the method
country-agnostic by construction.

**A caution from W03's research.** Country-agnostic in *design* is not country-agnostic in
*recognition*. Every Ghanaian regulator caps what an online programme can contribute to annual
CPD at 20–40%, and those caps are written against asynchronous self-paced study. If live
synchronous sessions sit outside the cap — which needs a written ruling — then the monthly live
session becomes the credit-bearing component, and this design already has one every month.

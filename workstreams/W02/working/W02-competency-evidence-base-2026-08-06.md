# What a healthcare quality professional is supposed to be able to do

Evidence base for the missing half of the QIPS design. Everything below carries a live URL and
an accessed date, or is written NOT FOUND with the searches recorded. All accessed 6 August 2026.

---

## Why this exists

The programme's canon contains twenty-nine facts. None of them says what a graduate can do.
The design walkthrough contains fifty-two stops. None of them asks. The curriculum spine carries
performance outcomes for months 1 and 2 and outlines only from month 3.

And canon states that host sites are qualified against a standard *"derived from programme
objectives"* — a document that is referenced in four places and exists in none of them.

Meanwhile the marketing copy already promises that a graduate can *"investigate harm and manage
risk"* and *"turn data into confident decisions"*. Those are competency claims the design does
not define, the spine does not deliver past month 2, and no check tests.

That is the gap. This is the evidence for closing it.

---

## 1. The three findings that change the question

### 1.1 There is no African-authored competency framework for healthcare quality professionals

This is the most consequential thing in this document, and it is corroborated twice, independently,
in peer-reviewed work published this year.

**Rathnayake et al., *International Journal for Quality in Health Care*, 2026** — ISQua's own
journal — screened 14,144 records and included 118 articles, of which thirty-six carried explicit
competency frameworks. Their Table 1 gives the country or region of publication for the full
included set:

> USA and Latin America — 31; Europe — 15; Canada — 13; Korea — 13; United Kingdom — 12;
> Australia and New Zealand — 10; Asia pacific region (without Korea) — 24

Those sum to 118. **Africa does not appear as a category at all.**
[doi.org/10.1093/intqhc/mzag090](https://doi.org/10.1093/intqhc/mzag090) ·
[PMC13322961](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13322961/)

**Eloranta et al., *Frontiers in Health Services*, 2026** compared the five patient-safety
competency models they judged worth comparing — the WHO curriculum guide, Australia's national
framework, the Canadian Safety Competencies, the NHS Patient Safety Syllabus, and the Nordic
Framework. Their stated limitation, verbatim:

> "A limitation of this review is that the four patient safety models apart from WHO Patient
> Safety Curriculum analysed originate rom high income regions." *[sic]*

And their recommendation, verbatim:

> "In future research, it will be important to evaluate patient safety competence models from the
> perspective of low and middle-income countries, to identify how local conditions, resource
> constraints, cultural practices, and healthcare system structures influence patient safety
> competence and education."

[doi.org/10.3389/frhs.2026.1757601](https://doi.org/10.3389/frhs.2026.1757601) ·
[PMC13079276](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13079276/)

**WHO has nothing that fills the gap.** Its Multi-professional Patient Safety Curriculum Guide
(2011) is a teaching curriculum for universities, not a professional competency framework, and the
Frontiers authors judge it *"Published in 2011, so some of the content may be outdated"*.
Its Global Competency Framework for Universal Health Coverage (2022) covers the general health
workforce across six domains — people-centredness, decision-making, communication, collaboration,
evidence-informed practice and personal conduct. Quality and patient safety is not one of them.
[WHO 2011](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241501958) ·
[WHO 2022](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240034686)

**What regional bodies have instead is facility standards, not professional competencies.** India's
NHSRC certifies facilities against National Quality Assurance Standards and runs training for
quality managers, but publishes no competency framework. SQHN publishes facility accreditation
standards. Neither describes what a quality professional should be able to do.

**What this means.** A Nigeria-authored, LMIC-grounded competency framework for healthcare quality
professionals would, on this evidence, be first of its kind — and two 2026 reviews have just named
that absence as the field's priority gap. This is the same shape as the QI 5.1 finding from the
pricing work: the most valuable action available is one that is entirely within SQHN's gift.

### 1.2 Employers do not ask for a quality credential. They ask for a licence, years, and a degree

Eighteen adverts across six countries. **One names a personal quality credential.**

That one — a Medical Quality Director post asking for CPHQ, FISQua and an IASSC/ASQ Black Belt —
is dated 21 March 2022, the employer is anonymous ("a reputable hospital in Lagos"), and it was
posted on SQHN's own careers page rather than any job board.
[sqhn.org/careers](https://sqhn.org/careers/)

Targeted searches for the credentials as hiring requirements returned no African adverts at all.
`"FISQua" OR "ISQua Fellowship" job advert requirement` returned no job adverts anywhere in the
world — only ISQua's own pages and training vendors.

**What they gate on instead, in order of consistency:**

*A clinical licence.* Garki Hospital Abuja: *"Candidates must possess an MBBS or its equivalent,
fully registered with MDCN and have a current practising license."* MDCN registration recurs as an
absolute requirement across Nigerian hospital adverts.
[Garki, posted 3 July 2025](https://www.myjobmag.com/job/director-of-clinical-governance-garki-hospital-abuja-gha)

*A postgraduate degree — and where quality-specific education is required, it is a degree, not a
certificate.* Garki again, and this is the strongest quality-specific education requirement found
anywhere in Nigeria: *"A postgraduate qualification in quality management, patient safety or
healthcare leadership is required."* Cedarcrest: *"A Master's in Healthcare Management/Quality is
an advantage."*

*Years of hospital experience, weighted heavily.* Garki asks for ten years clinical with five in a
leadership role. Cedarcrest: seven years. Evercare's junior post: one to three.

*Accreditation-system fluency, named as systems rather than as personal certification.* Evercare:
*"Certification in Quality Management, Patient Safety, or related accreditation systems (e.g.,
ISO, JCI, COHSASA) is desirable."* Cedarcrest asks only for *"Knowledge of ISO 9001, JCI, or other
healthcare accreditation standards."*

**A scarcity finding worth stating.** On the general Nigerian job boards — MyJobMag's Quality
Assurance category, HotNigerianJobs, Jobberman's Quality Control and Assurance category — the
combined listings on 6 August 2026 contained *zero* hospital clinical-quality roles. "Quality
Assurance" in Nigerian recruitment overwhelmingly means food, oil and gas, construction, pharma
manufacturing or software. Healthcare quality roles are found only by going to hospital employer
pages directly. The market this programme serves is not yet a visible labour market.

**And one negative finding that matters commercially.** In the HMO sector, "Quality Assurance"
means claims vetting. The Songhai Health Trust advert is entirely about bills, tariffs and
fourteen-day turnaround. There is no clinical quality content in it at all. HMOs should not be
counted as a buyer for clinical quality training on the evidence of their job titles.

### 1.3 The most common duty in the entire sample is teaching other people

Ten of the eighteen adverts ask the post-holder to train, mentor or build capability in other
staff. That is more than ask for audits (eight), more than ask for data and dashboards (eight),
more than ask for root cause analysis (five).

Cedarcrest: *"Train staff on quality standards, patient safety, and improvement tools like PDCA
and RCA."* Jhpiego: *"Lead preparation, planning and implementation of QI capacity-building
activities (training and mentoring)"* and *"Provide onsite and virtual support and mentoring to at
least 15 sites."*

**This is the design insight.** The programme currently produces someone who has *done* one
improvement project. What employers are actually buying is someone who can *build the capability
in everyone else* — and that is a different and harder claim, which the programme's own
internal-coaching model is already structured to support but does not state as an outcome.

## 2. What the field's frameworks actually contain

### NAHQ Healthcare Quality Competency Framework

Eight domains: Professional Engagement; Quality Leadership & Integration; Performance & Process
Improvement; Population Health & Care Transitions; Health Data Analytics; Patient Safety;
Regulatory & Accreditation; Quality Review & Accountability. Currently *"8 domains and 28
competencies available to all, as well as skill statements accessible through select NAHQ
programming"* — the skill statements are paywalled. A refresh was released in February 2026.
[nahq.org framework](https://nahq.org/why-nahq/healthcare-quality-competency-framework/)

Three proficiency tiers, defined in NAHQ's Professional Development Guide (©2023, so pre-refresh —
treat as the most recently published definitions rather than confirmed current):

> **Foundational** — "These professionals have a working knowledge of healthcare quality concepts
> if reference and/or context is provided. They can complete some tasks independently but need
> rules and references."
>
> **Proficient** — "…a deep understanding of healthcare quality concepts and a holistic approach to
> problem solving. They complete work independently with deliberate planning… They recognize
> relevance and variation and can connect common attributes and aspects of key issues."
>
> **Advanced** — "…a nuanced understanding… deal with routine matters and issues intuitively,
> perform a high standard of work independently, and can assess the competence of others… they can
> develop and communicate strategic vision."

[Professional Development Guide](https://nahq.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Professional-Development-Guide-4-LP.pdf)

The discriminators, in NAHQ's own terms, are *reference-dependence* (needs rules → independent with
planning → intuitive), *scope of independence*, *ability to assess the competence of others*
(Advanced only) and *strategic vision* (Advanced only).

NAHQ's own expectation, verbatim, and it is directly useful: *"All healthcare quality professionals
should be at the Foundational level in all 8 domains; however… it is standard for an individual's
level to vary across the Framework."* And: *"It is not necessary to be an Advanced level in all
areas."*

**Correction to anything that says otherwise: NAHQ does not publish percentage weightings for the
CPHQ exam.** It publishes item counts across seven sections totalling 125 scored items — Quality
Leadership and Integration 19, Performance and Process Improvement 27, Population Health and Care
Transitions 11, Health Data Analytics 26, Patient Safety 18, Quality Review and Accountability 16,
Regulatory and Accreditation 8. Any percentage table attributed to NAHQ is derived, not published.
[CPHQ content outline](https://cdn.nahq.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2024-CPHQ_Outline_R2b.pdf)

Note the asymmetry: the framework has eight domains, the exam has seven sections. **Professional
Engagement is not examined.**

### AAMC QIPS Competencies, Version 2 (2024)

Three domains: *Patients, Caregivers, and Health Care Professionals as Partners in QIPS*; *Patient
Safety*; *Quality Improvement*. Down from five in the 2019 edition — and the reason is a design
precedent worth borrowing: health equity was *"designated as a common, consistent thread through
all competencies, as opposed to being a separate domain"*.
[AAMC store, free PDF](https://store.aamc.org/quality-improvement-and-patient-safety-competencies-across-the-learning-continuum-version-2.html)

Its staged verb taxonomy is directly reusable for writing performance outcomes:

> Stage 1: "describe," "identify," "consider," or "discuss"; focus on self, foundational knowledge…
> Stage 2: "demonstrate" or "model"; focus on team, leadership, decision-making, and accountability.
> Stage 3: "coach," "lead," "mentor," "vision," "challenge," or "change"; focus on system, agency,
> change management, and enduring growth.

And an explicit exclusion this programme should note rather than ignore: *"the QIPS competencies
are not intended to be used for high-stakes assessment or accreditation of schools, programs, or
institutions."*

### IHI Certified Professional in Patient Safety

Four domains — Culture; Systems Thinking, Human Factors Engineering, and Design; Safety Risks and
Responses; Performance Measurement, Analysis, Improvement and Monitoring — with an unlabelled
column reading 20 / 20 / 35 / 25 which sums to 100 against a 100-scored-item exam. The source does
not label those numbers as percentages, so neither should we.
[CPPS exam content outline](https://241684.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/241684/CPPS%20Exam%20Content%20Outline%20Published%20Sept%202024.pdf)

Eligibility is real: a baccalaureate plus three years, or an associate degree plus five, in a
healthcare setting. NCCA-accredited. USD 549 domestic, **USD 649 international**.
[CPPS FAQ](https://www.ihi.org/learn/certifications/cpps/frequently-asked-questions)

## 3. Where the programme sits against all of it

| | Contact/study hours | Supervised practice | Assessment | Price to a Nigerian |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NAHQ HQ Concepts | 1 hour | none | none | USD 99 |
| NAHQ HQ Principles | 6 contact hours | none | none | USD 150 |
| ISQua Fellowship | 60–70 study hours | none | quizzes and reflections, no exam | **EUR 225** first year (LMIC band) |
| CPHQ | none — exam only | none | 125-item proctored, NCCA, pass 600/800 | **USD 649–799** |
| IHI CPPS | none — exam only | none | 120-item proctored, NCCA | **USD 649** |
| IHI Improvement Advisor | 11 months | own project | — | **USD 13,500** |
| **QIPS as designed** | **~228 hours** | **12 artefacts, 40-hour observership, capstone** | committee-governed | **~USD 500–600** (under review) |

Sources for each row are cited in section 2 and in the pricing dossier. The ISQua band is verbatim:
*"LMIC – Lower-Middle Income €225 / €150"* first year / renewal, membership included.
[isqua.org/fellowship](https://isqua.org/fellowship/)

**The empty space is real and the programme is already standing in it.** Nothing in the comparator
set combines substantial supervised practice with an LMIC-affordable price. ISQua's fellowship is
a fifth of the study hours with no supervised practice; the two credentials are exams with no
teaching at all; IHI's advisor programme is twenty-five times the price. QIPS at 228 hours of
structured practice for roughly USD 500 is not competing with any of them on their terms.

**And the tier this supports has to be stated honestly.** 228 hours cannot produce Proficient
across eight NAHQ domains — that would be a claim the design cannot carry, and an informed buyer
benchmarking against NAHQ will know it. What 228 hours with twelve artefacts, an observership and
a delivered capstone *can* support is a defensible claim of Foundational across the domains the
programme actually teaches, with something stronger — demonstrated, evidenced practice — in the
two or three it teaches deeply. That distinction is the design decision, and it belongs to the
committee.

## 4. NOT FOUND — recorded so nobody re-runs these searches

- **Any Nigerian advert requiring CPHQ, CPPS or FISQua as a condition of appointment.** Searched
  four query variants across MyJobMag, HotNigerianJobs, Jobberman and general web. The 2022 SQHN
  careers item is the only African hit.
- **Any Nigerian healthcare advert naming Lean or Six Sigma.** All results were US boards.
- **Current NAHQ tier definitions post the February 2026 refresh.** The refreshed page states the
  refresh happened but does not restate the tiers; skill statements are gated behind a lead-capture
  form. The 2026 *Journal for Healthcare Quality* article on the framework's evolution is paywalled
  on Ovid and is the next step if the exact refreshed wording becomes load-bearing.
- **ISQua Fellowship entry requirements.** No academic, professional or experience prerequisite is
  published anywhere. The page HTML was searched for "eligib", "requirement", "prerequisit" and
  "qualif" — zero hits. Do not state a prerequisite for it.
- **A competency framework from Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Pakistan, Sri Lanka or any African
  regional body.** Targeted searches per country returned facility standards, incident-reporting
  guidelines and academic commentary. Medium confidence per individual country; high confidence at
  field level, given both 2026 reviews independently found none.
- **Current Ghanaian healthcare quality adverts.** MyJobMag Ghana's medical listings are stale
  (newest item May 2024). The Ghana evidence in this document is thin and dated — treat it as
  indicative only.
- **South African private hospital group adverts** (Netcare, Mediclinic, Life Healthcare, Lenmed).
  Four query variants; Pnet's own healthcare quality category returned 25 listings with zero
  hospital clinical-quality roles. South Africa is the weakest leg of this evidence base.
- **Nigeria quality adviser adverts from MSH, FHI 360 or Clinton Health Access Initiative.** Only
  non-Nigeria roles surfaced. Jhpiego is the only international NGO for which live Nigerian quality
  adverts could be retrieved.

## 5. One caution on attribution

One advert circulating as *"Quality Assurance Manager at Reddington Hospital"* carries a company
field reading **Confidential** and body text saying only *"An established healthcare organization"*.
It should not be attributed to Reddington in front of a hospital director who may know them.

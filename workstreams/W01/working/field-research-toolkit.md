# W01 field research toolkit

Status: ready for human fielding after privacy and outreach approval

## Coverage target

This is a diversity target, not a statistically representative sample. Seek at least eight
practitioner conversations and four employer/sponsor conversations in each country. Cover public,
private and not-for-profit settings; tertiary, secondary and primary/community services; titled and
informal quality roles; clinical and non-clinical work; individual and institutional payment routes.

## Recruitment routes to verify at time of use

- SQHN and comparable national quality/patient-safety networks;
- ministries, hospital boards, teaching hospitals, private groups and faith-based networks;
- professional associations and postgraduate/health-management faculties;
- regulators, healthcare commissions, insurers/HMOs and accreditation teams;
- implementation partners and health-programme organisations;
- direct employer outreach and professional-network referrals.

Do not place names or contact details in GitHub or Base44. Track only aggregate invited, agreed and
completed counts here; restricted contact records stay in Drive.

## Consent opening

Explain the programme is being designed, participation is voluntary, no admission or benefit is
promised, responses may be summarised without identity, sensitive employer/patient details should
not be shared, and the person may stop or skip any question. Record consent version and decision.

## Practitioner interview

1. Describe the last quality or safety problem you personally worked on.
2. What tasks did you perform, and which were outside your formal role?
3. Where did you become stuck: authority, method, measurement, teamwork, data, time or resources?
4. What evidence or decision did your manager expect?
5. Which methods do you use confidently, with help or not at all?
6. What learning have you tried, paid or free? What transferred into practice?
7. Could you access a workplace problem, responsible contact and non-identifiable data for a year?
8. What weekly rhythm, live timing, connectivity and accessibility needs would be workable?
9. What would make an observership useful or unsafe?
10. Which award or recognition matters, and why?
11. Who would pay, approve time and bear travel? What would stop them?
12. What would make you leave before month 12?

## Employer/sponsor interview

Ask for the quality tasks the organisation needs, current performance gaps, evidence expected from
staff, available project/data access, protected time, sponsorship decision, procurement path,
observership/host capacity, recognition requirements and reasons a graduate would or would not be
trusted with greater responsibility.

## Coding sheet

For each interview code tasks, current proficiency, consequence, frequency, learning substitute,
workplace access, support, constraints, purchasing route, recognition need and disconfirming
evidence. Separate direct quotation in restricted notes from de-identified analytic codes.

## Decision outputs

Produce a task-frequency × proficiency-gap map, country/setting differences, persona revisions,
substitute map, sponsor route, completion risks and a traceable “supported / contradicted / unknown”
status for every hypothesis. Percentages always show numerator and denominator.

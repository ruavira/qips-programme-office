# W01 persona hypotheses and segmentation

Status: hypotheses for field testing; not approved personas or eligibility rules

## Segmentation variables

Record country, care setting, public/private/not-for-profit context, profession, quality tasks,
formal authority, team access, data access, workplace problem access, sponsor/equivalent support,
weekly time, connectivity, ability-to-pay route, career intent and required recognition. Do not
collect patient information.

## Five hypotheses

### P1 — frontline improvement lead

A clinician or allied professional asked to fix a ward, service or safety problem while quality work
is only part of the role. Likely need: methods, measurement, influence without authority and a
credible project pathway. Test whether protected time and data access exist.

### P2 — dedicated quality or clinical-governance practitioner

Already owns audits, incidents, standards, indicators or accreditation readiness. Likely need:
systems thinking, improvement beyond compliance, advanced measurement and executive influence.
Test proficiency rather than assuming the word “quality” means advanced practice.

### P3 — facility or network decision-maker

A medical director, administrator, nursing leader, payer/network manager or programme lead who must
make quality visible and govern improvement across teams. Likely need: governance, portfolio
prioritisation, measurement, risk and sponsorship capability. Test whether a 12-month participant
role or an institutional sponsor route is the better fit.

### P4 — oversight and implementation professional

A regulator, inspector, HMO, ministry, NGO or development-programme professional who defines,
assesses or supports quality across organisations. Likely need: consistent judgement, implementation
methods and learning across settings. Test conflict and observership feasibility.

### P5 — career transitioner

An early-career health graduate or experienced professional moving into quality without a formal
quality portfolio. Likely need: foundations, supervised evidence and career signalling. Test whether
the person can access a real improvement problem, data and responsible workplace contact; do not
promise employment or admit solely for aspiration.

## Cross-cutting exclusions from inference

Country, profession, seniority, employer prestige and ability to pay do not by themselves establish
need, readiness, selection priority or likely completion.

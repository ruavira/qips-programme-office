## HEADLINE FINDING (read this first)

**Pakistan has no operating national CPD accreditation scheme that a Nigerian body can enter, and no profession in Pakistan currently requires CPD for licence renewal.** There is exactly **one** live, documented, priced route with a published lead time — **CPSP Joint Providership** — and its published eligibility text limits it to *"[a]ny institute of the country"* (i.e. Pakistan). The route is **per-activity, not per-programme**, requires a **CPSP department or specialist faculty to be the host collaborator**, and **awards no credit at all for enduring/asynchronous e-learning** — which is the bulk of a hybrid programme.

**The lead-time answer: 8 weeks per activity → latest lodgement 14 November 2026** (see §C). But the binding constraint is not the 8 weeks; it is securing a CPSP internal faculty sponsor first, and that has no published timeline.

### 1. PMDC — Pakistan Medical and Dental Council

**Which body currently regulates, and under what law.**
The regulator is the **Pakistan Medical and Dental Council**, re-established by the **Pakistan Medical and Dental Council Act, 2022**, published in *The Gazette of Pakistan, Extraordinary, 16 January 2023*. The Act **dissolved the Pakistan Medical Commission** (s.51(3): *"The dissolved Pakistan Medical Commission as formed pursuant to the repealed Act stands dissolved upon the commencement of this Act"*) and expressly repealed all regulations made under the PMC Act (s.51(2) proviso). PMDC's own site styles it *"(Successor of Pakistan Medical Commission)"*.
- [OWN][PDF] https://pmdc.pk/Documents/law/PMDC%20Act.pdf — accessed 2026-08-01, HTTP 200, 2,410,715 bytes, text extracted locally.
- [3P] https://pakistancode.gov.pk/english/UY2FqaJw1-apaUY2Fqa-apaUY2Npa5lkaQ==-sg-jjjjjjjjjjjjj — accessed 2026-08-01 (index page only; the text is in the linked PDF).

**Is CME/CPD mandatory for licence renewal? NO.**
The **PM&DC Registration Regulations 2023** (dated 04/12/2023 on the Council's Laws & Regulations page) are the operative instrument for licence renewal. I downloaded and text-searched the full document: it contains **zero occurrences** of "CPD", "continuous professional development", "continuing medical education" or "CME". It provides only:
- *"Renewal of License with Basic Qualification (5 years) — Rs. 10,000/-"*
- *"Renewal of License with Additional Postgraduate Qualification (5 years) — Rs. 10,000/-"*
- Late fee: *"Rs. 2,000/year"* after expiry of six months after validity.
(Currency: Pakistani Rupees; period: per 5-year licence cycle.)
- [OWN][PDF] https://pmdc.pk/Documents/law/Registration%20Regulations%202023.pdf — accessed 2026-08-01, HTTP 200.
- [OWN] https://pmdc.pk/Publication/LawsAndRegulations — accessed 2026-08-01. Five instruments listed; **no CPD or CME regulation exists**.

**Historic position (flagged).** **[STALE]** A 2019 peer-reviewed study reports: *"Although there is an established code to complete 30 hours of CME/5 years, only doctors in teaching hospitals comply with this, as it is not legally binding"* and *"Fifteen credit hours for general practitioners and 30 credit hours for specialists."* This predates both the 2020 PMC Act and the 2022 PMDC Act and is **not** reflected in the current regulations. [3P] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6500816/ — accessed 2026-08-01, published Mar–Apr 2019. **[STALE]**
**[STALE]** A 2011 AKU paper: *"no system has yet been developed to measure and certify physician's participation in educational events."* [3P][PDF] https://ecommons.aku.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1003&context=pakistan_fhs_mc_cpe — accessed 2026-08-01, October 2011.
A 2024 peer-reviewed case study of CME providers states: *"Pakistan has made numerous attempts to establish and implement a national mandatory CME program which currently do not exist"* and cites *"the absence of a CME unit at the Pakistan Medical and Dental Council"* as a barrier. [3P] https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-024-05201-y — accessed 2026-08-01, published 12 March 2024.

**Does PMDC accredit CPD providers? The statutory route — and the fatal clause.**
Section 27 of the PMDC Act 2022, quoted verbatim from the Gazette text:

> **27. Recognition of continuous professional development opportunity providers.—** (1) The Federal Government through Minister-in-charge may grant recognition to **any organization in Pakistan**, which provides opportunities of continuous professional development to the registered medical and dental practitioners. All such recognized organizations shall be included in the Fifth Schedule by the Federal Government through Minister-in-charge by notification in the official Gazette.
> (2) If **any organization in Pakistan**, which provides opportunities of continuous professional development to the registered medical and dental practitioners, intends to get recognition of the organization in pursuance of sub-section (1) it shall apply to the Federal Government through Minister-in-charge for the same.
> (3) The Federal Government through Minister-in-charge shall transmit the application to the Council after being satisfied that application is complete and is in accordance with the prescribed procedure. The Council shall form recommendations for the Federal Government through Minister-in-charge after assessing the institution and the qualification as per prescribed procedure. Upon receiving recommendations from the Council, the Federal Government through Minister-in-charge shall grant recognition as provided under sub-section (1), declaring that it shall be a recognized organization **in Pakistan** …
> (4) A separate list of continuous professional development opportunities providing organizations shall be maintained by the Council.

Reinforcing provisions: s.2(e) defines a CPD provider as *"an organization providing continuous professional development opportunities and included in the Fifth Schedule"*; s.34 makes it an offence to run *"an organization for continuous professional development opportunity providing"* without recognition; s.31 and s.33 give the Council information and withdrawal powers over CPD providers.

**Contrast to prove the point:** where the Act intends to reach outside Pakistan it says so. s.2(f) and s.2(j) define dental/medical institutions as *"within or outside Pakistan"*. Section 27 does not. **The territorial limit in s.27 is deliberate drafting, not an oversight.**

**Fifth Schedule status.** The Act's schedule pages (Gazette pp. 46–55) are **scanned images in the published PDF with no extractable text**; I could not read the entries. I found no Gazette notification recognising any CPD provider under s.27. **NOT FOUND** — see register.

**Prescribed procedure, form, fee, lead time.** s.27(3) refers to a *"prescribed procedure"*. No such regulation has been made: the Council's Laws & Regulations page lists only Admissions (2025), Assessment (2025), Teaching (2025), Registration (2023) and PG (2023) regulations. **NOT FOUND** — no application form, no fee, no service standard, no committee calendar.

**What is actually happening.** On 1 July 2025 PMDC announced it is *developing* a CPD regulatory system with **ACCME**. A 2.5-day strategy session was held in Islamabad with ACCME President Dr Graham McMahon and VP Accreditation Dr Dion Richetti, and the Federal Health Minister. Reporting is explicit that **no operational dates, phases, credit requirements or provider-accreditation mechanism were announced.**
- [3P] https://www.dawn.com/news/1921367/pmdc-partners-with-us-based-council-to-develop-regulatory-system — accessed 2026-08-01, published 1 July 2025.
- [3P] https://www.brecorder.com/news/40370430/medicaldental-education-pmdc-accme-developing-cpd-regulatory-system — accessed 2026-08-01, published 1 July 2025.
- [3P] https://pk.dental-tribune.com/news/pmdc-to-implement-global-cpd-framework-for-dentists-and-doctors-in-pakistan/ — accessed 2026-08-01, published 2 July 2025.
I searched for any 2026 launch, notification or credit-hour announcement and found **none**. As of 1 August 2026 — thirteen months after the announcement — **there is nothing to apply to**.

**PMDC verdict for SQHN: closed.** Statutorily barred to a Nigerian body; no scheme operating; no lead time computable.

### 2. Pakistan Nursing and Midwifery Council (PNMC)

**Law.** Pakistan Nursing Council Act 1973 (Act XXVI of 1973, assented 9 Feb 1973), as amended by the **Pakistan Nursing and Midwifery Council (Amendment) Act, 2023** (Act V of 2023), published in *The Gazette of Pakistan, Extraordinary, 21 January 2023*.
- [OWN] https://pnmc.gov.pk/pnmc-act/ — accessed 2026-08-01.
- [OWN][PDF] https://pnmc.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/PNC-Act-1973.pdf and .../PNMC-Amended-Act-2023.pdf — both accessed 2026-08-01, HTTP 200, downloaded and text-searched.

**CPD mandatory? NO.** I text-searched both Acts for "CPD", "continuing", "continuous professional", "renewal", "refresher" — **zero hits in either**. The Council's own online-services page states renewal is *"every 5 years' interval from the date of Registration"*, fee **Rs. 2,000** (PKR, per 5-year renewal), by demand draft payable to *"The Registrar, Pakistan Nursing and Midwifery Council"*, submitted by post or in person; *"faxed/emailed applications are not accepted"*. **No CPD condition is stated.**
- [OWN] https://online.pnmc.gov.pk/requirement-procedures — accessed 2026-08-01.

**Does PNMC accredit CPD? No — it accredits institutions.** New s.3A(h) of the amended Act empowers the Council *"to evaluate and accredit institutions that provide education and training in nursing, midwifery and LHV, and to cooperate with other accreditation bodies where necessary"*, and s.3A(k)(i) covers *"standards for education, clinical practice, professional conduct and institutional accreditation"*. There is **no activity-level CPD credit scheme, no credit-hour currency and no CPD-provider register**.

**What PNMC actually offers members.** Free CPD content through the **World Continuing Education Alliance (WCEA)**, described on PNMC's own page as *"FREE online internationally accredited Professional Training Courses for registered Nursing and Midwifery Professionals"* accessed via the WCEA mobile app; registration at engagement.wcea.education/pnc. The WCEA landing page states PNMC acts *"in collaboration with the WCEA (partner of the International Council of Nurses)"*. **No published route for a third party to submit content for PNMC approval, no fee, no lead time** — NOT FOUND.
- [OWN] https://pnmc.gov.pk/cpd-courses/ — accessed 2026-08-01.
- [3P] https://engagement.wcea.education/pnc?source_code=WS01 — accessed 2026-08-01.

**The nearest available route (institutional recognition) and why it doesn't fit.** PNMC's published requirements for a new institute: *"Infrastructure, Human Resources, Equipment, Clinical Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with a Hospital"*; documents *"signed by owner with seal of institute"*; then inspection; then Council approval. Fees (PKR, one-off):
- Application processing: **Rs. 50,000**
- Inspection: **Rs. 2,50,000** (250,000)
- Programme registration (degree): **Rs. 2,00,000** per programme
- Programme registration (diploma): **Rs. 1,00,000** per programme
- Institute recognition: **Rs. 1,00,000**

**No processing time, inspection window or Council decision timeline is published anywhere on the page** — NOT FOUND. This route recognises **nursing education institutes** delivering PNMC curricula, requires **physical infrastructure in Pakistan and a hospital MOU**, and is not designed for a 12-month CPD programme delivered by a foreign society.
- [OWN] https://pnmc.gov.pk/rules-for-new-institutes/ — accessed 2026-08-01.
- [OWN] https://pnmc.gov.pk/fee/ — accessed 2026-08-01. **Fee chart is published as an image only**; amounts not machine-readable from the page text.

**PNMC verdict for SQHN: no CPD route. Institutional route requires Pakistani premises and a hospital MOU; timeline unpublished.**

### 3. Pharmacy Council of Pakistan and provincial pharmacy councils

**Law.** Pharmacy Act 1967 (as amended by the Pharmacy Amendment Act 1973). The federal PCP registers pharmacists and recognises institutions; **provincial** councils (Punjab, Sindh, KP, Balochistan) hold the registers and do renewals.

**CPD mandatory? NO — and this is on the record in a Dec-2025 peer-reviewed paper:**
> *"In most countries, CPD is mandatory for re-licensure, but in Pakistan it remains voluntary."*
The same paper identifies mandatory CPD as one of five urgent unmet priorities and notes PCP's authority has been *"weakened"* by *"fragmentation following devolution"*.
- [3P] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12777864/ — "Strengthening Pakistan's pharmaceutical workforce: from evidence to action", accessed 2026-08-01, published 24 December 2025.

**Federal PCP.** Its site carries programme recognition (Pharm D, Pharmacy Technician, Postgraduate), institution lists, and notifications. **No CPD/continuing-education content, no licence-renewal procedure, no CPD-provider accreditation.** Its FAQ page lists 15 questions covering programme fees, NOC procedure, recognition-vs-accreditation and Good Standing Certificates — and contains **no CPD requirement**. Programme fees quoted there are institutional annual fees (*"Pharm.D Program is Rs.300,000/-"*, *"Category-B Program is Rs.200,000/-"* — PKR, per year), not CPD fees.

### 4. CPSP — College of Physicians and Surgeons Pakistan **(the only live route)**

**Status.** Not a licensing regulator. It is a postgraduate college that operates Pakistan's only functioning accredited-CME system. It states it is *"accredited as a CME provider by the Accreditation Council for Continuing Medical Education (ACCME)"* and that *"CME Credits issued by the CPSP shall be accepted internationally"*, describing itself as the first institution outside North America and Europe to hold this. ACCME's own eligibility rules confirm that *"Organizations located or incorporated outside the US or its territories"* may be accredited (with a requirement to attend an ACCME live educational event in person before an initial application) — so the claim is structurally credible. I could **not** independently confirm current standing in ACCME's provider directory: https://www.accme.org/find-a-cme-provider?keyword=Pakistan returned **HTTP 403**, and https://www.accme.org/accredited-provider-search?search=Pakistan returned **HTTP 404** (both 2026-08-01). Treat CPSP's accreditation as **[OWN]-asserted, not third-party verified**.
- [OWN] https://www.cpsp.edu.pk/cme.php and https://www.cpsp.edu.pk/cme-message.php — accessed 2026-08-01.
- [3P] https://accme.org/about-accreditation/expectations-eligibility/ — accessed 2026-08-01.

**Two categories of accredited activity.**
- **Category A — in-house CPSP activities.** Pre-Activity Form *"To be submitted 4 weeks before the activity"* (current form) / *"at least two weeks before"* (booklet and website). Reading material *"to be submitted atleast 2 weeks in advance"*. Post-activity report within one week (form) / two weeks (booklet).
- **Category B — Joint Provider Clinical Activities.** This is the external route.

**Category B eligibility — the decisive sentence.** CPSP's own CME Activities page states:
> *"**Any institute of the country** can collaborate with CPSP in the organizing of a CME activity."*

And the booklet defines Category B as *"Activities organized by the CPSP departments/specialist faculties in collaboration with an organization/institution other than CPSP"* — i.e. **a CPSP department or specialist faculty must be the organising host**, and the application must name *"Name of focal person of CPSP department/faculty"*. A Nigerian society cannot be the applicant in its own right; it must find an internal CPSP sponsor.
- [OWN] https://www.cpsp.edu.pk/cme-activities.php — accessed 2026-08-01.
- [OWN][PDF] https://www.cpsp.edu.pk/files/program/cme/CME-Booklet.pdf — accessed 2026-08-01. **[STALE]** — the booklet records ACCME accreditation in July 2016 with policies effective *"from August 2016"* and is signed by Prof. Zafar Ullah Chaudhry as President. It remains the document currently published on the live site.

**LEAD TIME — published, and there is a conflict on the face of the documents.**
| Source | Requirement | Date/version |

| **Joint-Provider Pre-Activity Form (current, live on site)** | *"PRE-ACTIVITY FORM FOR INITIATING A CLINICAL ACTIVITY — **To be submitted 8 weeks prior to the activity**"* | Undated form, live 2026-08-01 |

**Plan on 8 weeks.** The form is the operative instrument and is the more demanding of the two. There is **no separate published decision service standard** — no "we will decide within X days", no committee calendar. The pre-submission deadline **is** the lead time by design: the activity date is fixed in the application and CPSP approves against it. **NOT FOUND:** any published turnaround from submission to written approval.

**Sequence and gates (from the booklet, Category B):** proposal + Pre-Application Request Form + disclosure forms for every planner/organiser/speaker + CVs + minute-to-minute schedule + proposed registration fee → **scrutinised by DME** for commercial/conflict of interest → if a conflict is found, referred to the **Technical Committee of CME** (an unscheduled additional step, no timeline published) → *"sent to the competent authority for final approval"* of the date, the processing fee and the registration fee → DME advertises the activity on the CPSP website and co-organises it. **Note the hard gate:** *"The request will not be processed unless bank draft of the processing fee is deposited in favour of CPSP."* The clock does not start until a Pakistani-rupee bank draft is physically in CPSP's hands.

**FEES (published in full — PKR and USD as given by CPSP; per activity):**
- Request processing fee — **activity of a day: Rs. 10,000/- ($100)**; **two days: Rs. 20,000/- ($200)**; **more than 2 days: Rs. 40,000/- ($400)**
- **Share in income from registration: 10%** (payable with the post-activity report — the joint-provider Post-Activity requirement states *"along with 10% of total registration fee collected"*)
- Venue: **Rs. 10,000 per day** if CPSP premises are used
- Airtime: **Rs. 5,000 per day** for video-linked activities
- Co-provider bears all other costs: speaker travel and lodging, food and beverages, transportation, miscellaneous.

**CREDIT RULES.**
- **1 credit per contact hour.** *"Participation in all live activities: shall carry one (1) AMA PRA Category 1 Credit™ for one hour of educational activity"*; and *"60 minutes of physician participation in a certified live activity equals one (1) AMA PRA Category 1 Credit™; credit is designated in 15 minute or 0.25 credit increments"*, rounded to the nearest quarter hour. (Historic note **[STALE]**: CPSP previously awarded *"one CME credit for two hours of activity"* before adopting the AMA system.)
- **Simultaneous sessions counted once** toward the designated maximum.

**ONLINE / BLENDED — THIS IS THE CAP THAT MATTERS.**
- **Live internet webinars DO count.** *"A live activity… Participation may be in person or remotely as is the case of teleconferences or **live Internet webinars**."* CPSP's own activity list includes courses delivered *"Face to face"* and *"Online"*.
- **Enduring/asynchronous material does NOT count.** *"An enduring material is a certified CME activity that endures over a specified time. These include print, audio, video and Internet materials, such as monographs, podcasts, CD-ROMs, DVDs, **archived webinars, as well as other web-based activities**. **CPSP presently does not plan to award credits to this category of activities**, hence its details are being skipped."*
> **Implication for SQHN:** the self-paced eLearning spine of a 12-month hybrid programme earns **zero** CPSP credit. Only synchronous sessions — live in-person or live webinar — are creditable, and only if each one is separately applied for 8 weeks ahead.

**TEACHING / RESOURCE-PERSON CREDIT — YES.**
> *"Faculty credit: Accredited CME providers may also award AMA PRA Category 1 Credit™ to their physician faculty to recognize the learning associated with the preparation and teaching of an original presentation… Physician faculty may be awarded credit based on a **2:1 ratio to presentation time**. For example, faculty may be awarded 2 AMA PRA Category 1 Credit™ for a one-hour presentation or 1.5… for a 45-minute presentation… **Credit may only be claimed once for an original presentation; credit may not be claimed for subsequent presentations of the same material.**"*
That last sentence bites a cohort programme: a faculty member repeating the same module to cohort 2 earns nothing.

**MUST AN ACTIVITY BE ACCREDITED BEFORE DELIVERY? YES, absolutely.**
> *"Be certified for AMA PRA Category 1 Credit™ **in advance of the activity; i.e. an activity may not be retroactively approved for credit**."*
There is no retrospective route. A session delivered without prior approval is permanently uncreditable.

**NON-CLINICAL CONTENT IS EXPLICITLY PERMITTED** — directly relevant to a quality-and-safety curriculum:
> *"Accredited CME providers may certify **non-clinical subjects (e.g. office management, patient-physician communications, faculty development)** for AMA PRA Category 1 Credit™ as long as these are appropriate to a physician audience and benefit the profession, patient care or public health."*

**Other credit rules for completeness:** journal-based CME = 1 credit per article; manuscript review = 3 credits; test-item writing = 10 credits.

**5b. Sindh Healthcare Commission (SHCC Act 2013).**
Registers and licenses HCEs under s.13; runs a Directorate of Clinical Governance & Training which reports having trained *"3658 PHCS/Clinics, 676 Hospitals, 824 Matabs, 1232 Homeopathic Clinics"* from June 2018 to March 2026, and states *"SHCC will facilitate HCEs by training them on particular standards."* **It trains establishments itself; it does not accredit trainers.** Its FAQ set on Clinical Governance & Training contains nothing on CPD-provider accreditation, staff CPD requirements, fees or application routes.

**5c. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Health Care Commission (KP HCC Act 2015).**
**s.6(2)(d):** *"play technical and advisory, educative and disciplinary role to support the registered and licensed health care establishments to improve quality of services"*. **s.6(2)(h):** *"impose and collect fees and charges on registration, licensing or for any other services rendered to health care establishments, **such as trainings on guidelines, standards, etc.**"* — i.e. the Commission **sells its own training**; it has **no accreditation power over external trainers** and imposes no CPD requirement on individuals.

### 6. CPD accreditation for health managers and non-clinical quality professionals

**No Pakistani body accredits CPD for health managers or non-clinical quality professionals.** There is no regulator for these roles: no register, no licence, therefore no CPD requirement and no accreditation machinery. Searches recorded in the NOT FOUND register.

What exists instead is a **private, unregulated market**:
- **PIQC Institute of Quality** — Certified Healthcare Quality Professional (CHQP), *"a special program of PIQC offering in Lahore with the collaboration of Shaukat Khanum Memorial Trust (SKMT), Lahore"*, *"spread over four months"*, curriculum covering PHC and JCI standards. **No claim of regulatory recognition; no fees published; no CPD credit claim.** [3P] https://piqc.edu.pk/healthcare-quality-professional-chqp/ — accessed 2026-08-01.
- **CPSP MCPS-HCSM** — a formal postgraduate membership qualification in Health Care Systems Management (upgraded to MCPS in 2014). This is a **qualification**, not a CPD accreditation service.
- **Pakistan Institute of Management** offers a "Diploma in Quality in Healthcare, Clinical Governance and SHCC Standards" (Karachi) — private, referencing SHCC standards without SHCC accreditation.
- **Proposed, not operating:** a 2025 Delphi study developed 26 proposed CPD standards for allied health professionals in Pakistan, aligned to WFME, conducted at the University of Lahore Jan–Jun 2024. These are **research outputs recommending a framework**, not an operating scheme, and name no accrediting body. [3P] https://pjms.org.pk/index.php/pjms/article/view/11585 — *Pakistan Journal of Medical Sciences* 41(10):2916–2924, published 29 September 2025, DOI 10.12669/pjms.41.10.11585 — accessed 2026-08-01. (The PMC mirror https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12616322/ returned a **Google reCAPTCHA challenge page**, not content, on 2026-08-01.)

**Consequence for SQHN's most likely Pakistani audience.** For the non-clinical quality professionals who are the programme's core market, there is **no regulator to seek recognition from, and none to be blocked by**. A programme-completion certificate stands on its own reputation. That is simultaneously the risk (no external validation) and the opportunity (no gatekeeper, no fee, no lead time).

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE COUNTRY DECISION — five sentences

1. **No Pakistani profession requires CPD for licence renewal today**, so CPD recognition buys SQHN far less market pull in Pakistan than in a mandatory-CPD jurisdiction — the value proposition has to rest on the certificate and the employer demand created by provincial hospital standards, not on regulatory necessity.
2. **The two statutory routes that could theoretically matter are both territorially closed:** PMDC s.27 says *"any organization in Pakistan"* three times, and CPSP says *"[a]ny institute of the country"* — Pakistan will require a **local partner as the named applicant**, not merely as a delivery agent.
3. **CPSP is the only live route, and it is per-activity at 8 weeks' notice, priced at Rs 10,000–40,000 per activity plus a 10% cut of registration income, with no credit whatsoever for asynchronous e-learning** — meaning the hybrid programme's eLearning spine is structurally uncreditable and only the live blocks can carry credit.
4. **The latest lodgement date for the first creditable session is 14 November 2026**, but the real gate is finding and signing a CPSP department or specialist faculty as co-host, which has no published timeline, so the go/no-go decision realistically has to be taken between **8 August and early October 2026**.
5. **For the programme's most likely Pakistani audience — non-clinical quality professionals and health managers — there is no regulator at all**, which removes both the gatekeeper and the validation, and is the cleanest argument for entering Pakistan on a partnership footing (a CPSP co-hosted live series plus an uncredited eLearning spine) rather than seeking provider status in SQHN's own name.

## The answer to the question asked

**The country decision must exist by 1 September 2026 — thirty days from today.**

It is not gated by the closest deadline. It is gated by the earliest deadline of any country that might
still be included, because you cannot wait and see. That earliest deadline belongs to Ghana.

| Route | Latest lodgement | Basis | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ghana — Pharmacy Council** | **~30 Sep 2026** | CPD Technical Committee sits **February, June and October only**. The October 2026 sitting is the last before cohort 1. The next is February 2027 — after the programme has started. | Meeting months published [OWN]. Papers-closing date NOT FOUND. |
| Ghana — Allied Health Professions Council | **1–9 Oct 2026**, a nine-day window | Two rules stack: applications "in the last quarter of every year" **and** "three months prior to execution". | Both rules published [OWN]. Which governs for a January start is NOT FOUND. |
| Ghana — Medical and Dental Council | 9 Oct 2026 | "Submit applications 3 months before the proposed date of the event." | Published [OWN]. No decision service standard exists. |
| **Nigeria — MDCN** | **9 Oct 2026** | *"Accreditation of a Provider shall not take more than three (3) months from the time of initial application to the granting of licence."* | Published maximum, verbatim [OWN]. |
| Nigeria — MLSCN | 9 Nov 2026 | "At least two months before the intended start of the event", per activity. | Published [OWN]. |
| Nigeria — NMCN | **unbounded — lodge first** | No service standard, no processing time, no meeting calendar published anywhere. Four sequential gates including a physical inspection visit and a Council representative attending the first programme. | NOT FOUND. |
| Nigeria — PCN | not applicable | No third-party provider route found at all. | Inferred from a gazetted fee schedule that prices provider accreditation for pharmacy schools but has no MCPD provider line. |
| Pakistan — CPSP | 14 Nov 2026 | 8 weeks per activity. | Published. But see below — the route may not be open to us at all. |

**Working back from 30 September.** Ghana's Medical and Dental Council Form B requires, for each event, a
full hourly programme, a complete speaker list with credentials and institutional affiliations, five
measurable learning objectives, teaching methods and an evaluation methodology. Nigeria's MDCN requires
a corporate registration certificate, the chief executive's medical degree and current practising
licence, and full credentials for three to four medically qualified resource persons. Four regulators,
two of which require two separate applications each.

Four weeks to assemble that is not generous. **30 September minus four weeks is 2 September.**

And it assumes the curriculum and the named faculty are already fixed — because MDC Form B cannot be
completed without a confirmed hourly programme and named speakers. **They are not fixed.** W02 is
authorised to prototype months 1 and 2, and W05's faculty slots have no consented names against them
(Q003). So the honest position is that the pack cannot be fully assembled by 30 September for Ghana,
whatever the committee decides.

## Three findings the committee should weigh before deciding F020

### 1. Pakistan has almost nothing to offer this programme on CPD, and may not admit us at all

**No Pakistani health profession currently requires CPD for licence renewal.** The PMDC's statutory power
to run a scheme exists under section 27 of the PMDC Act 2022, and an ACCME co-design was announced in
July 2025, but no scheme is operating. The 15-and-30-hours-per-five-years figure that circulates comes
from a 2019 paper describing a code that was, in its own words, *"not legally binding"* — and it predates
two subsequent restructurings of the regulator.

The single live, priced, documented route is **CPSP Joint Providership**, and three things about it
matter. Its published eligibility is *"any institute of the country"* — that is, Pakistan. It is
**per-activity, not per-programme**. And it **awards no credit whatsoever for enduring or asynchronous
e-learning**, which is the bulk of what this programme delivers.

So the CPD rationale for including Pakistan in cohort 1 is close to zero. There may still be a market
rationale — the pricing work put Pakistan's committed self-pay band at USD 270–540, which USD 500 sits
inside — but it should be argued on that basis, not on a recognition basis that does not exist.

### 2. Ghana is expensive in a way nobody has priced

The Allied Health Professions Council's published protocols require providers to remit **50% of gross
proceeds** where the Council fully facilitates, or **25% of gross proceeds** where it supplies
certificates and a supervisor. Which bracket an independently-run programme falls into is not addressed
in the document and is NOT FOUND.

Nigeria's NMCN separately requires **5% of total income remitted to Council** — from a 2017 guideline
still published as current, so the figure needs written confirmation.

**A 25–50% gross-proceeds levy is a pricing decision, not an administrative one.** F024 was ratified
yesterday at USD 450–600 with a working figure of USD 500. That figure was derived without any knowledge
of a regulator taking a quarter to a half of the gross. If Ghana is included and AHPC recognition is
sought, the viability arithmetic has to be redone.

### 3. The online cap is the finding that could reshape the product

Every Ghanaian regulator caps what an online course can contribute to a practitioner's annual
requirement:

| Body | Annual requirement | Maximum from online | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medical and Dental Council (doctors, dentists) | 15 credits | 4 | **27%** |
| Allied Health Professions Council (practitioners) | 20 points | 4 | **20%** |
| Pharmacy Council | 10 credits | 25% | **25%** |
| Nursing and Midwifery Council (staff nurse) | 20 points | 10 | **50%** [STALE, July 2020] |

Nigeria's MRTB caps online at 5 of 30 points. Nigeria's MDCN publishes no cap and names e-learning as a
recognised category — but is silent rather than permissive, which is not the same thing.

**A mostly-online programme cannot honestly be marketed in Ghana as meeting annual CPD requirements.** It
can be positioned as a contribution. That is a marketing constraint with legal edges, and it lands
directly on W14.

**But there is a lever, and it is worth more than any other single question in this file.** Every cap
found is written against *"online courses / distance learning with certificates"* or *"completion of an
online eLearning course"* — phrasings that describe **asynchronous, self-paced** study. Meanwhile Ghana's
MDC publishes a live 2026 schedule of 283 accredited programmes that includes webinar series carrying
ordinary programme credits at ordinary rates, and counts up to 6 hours and 5 credits per day.

If **live synchronous sessions are accredited as ordinary events, outside the asynchronous cap**, then
restructuring so that the credit-bearing component is the monthly live faculty session rather than the
eLearning release transforms the Ghanaian proposition — without changing F003, because the monthly cycle
already contains exactly that live session.

**This is inference, not a found rule.** It needs a written ruling from Ghana's MDC and AHPC. It should
be asked this week, because the answer may change what the applications describe.

## A fourth finding, on the strategy itself

Item 2 approved a completion certificate plus **CPD recognition pursued profession by profession from
named bodies**. The research says that strategy is materially weaker in practice than it looked on paper.

- **There is no Nigerian body that accredits CPD for health managers or non-clinical quality
  professionals.** Four separate searches found none. For precisely the audience a QIPS programme exists
  to serve, there is no recognition route to seek in the anchor market.
- Nigeria's PCN appears to run its MCPD centrally in cycles rather than accrediting outside providers,
  so pharmacists may be unreachable too.
- Nigeria's NMCN caps what a private provider can supply: **half of a nurse's three-year requirement must
  come from Council-organised programmes**.
- Ghana's MDC Form B states that *"events aimed primarily at training grade physicians, residents or non
  medical health professionals do not qualify for verifiable CPD credit approval."* A deliberately
  multi-professional cohort risks being read as exactly that.

None of this invalidates the Item 2 decision — the decoupling it built in, where CPD approvals are
independently releasable and never gate the award, is exactly what protects the programme here. But it
does mean **the completion certificate carries more of the value proposition than was assumed**, and
that W14 must not imply CPD recognition that may never arrive.

## What is proposed to canon

One amendment, to F020's `decide_by`:

> **The earlier of (a) 1 September 2026, being four weeks before the last Ghana Pharmacy Council CPD
> Technical Committee sitting that could decide before cohort 1 — the Committee meets only in February,
> June and October, and the February 2027 sitting falls after the programme starts — or (b) before
> applications open. Established by W03 on 2026-08-02 under W03-01 condition 6.**

If the committee resolves F020 to **Nigeria only**, the binding date relaxes to **9 October 2026**, set by
MDCN's published three-month maximum. That is worth stating plainly, because it means a decision to
narrow is also a decision to buy five weeks.

## What W03 could not establish

- NMCN's lead time. No service standard, processing time or meeting calendar is published anywhere. This
  is the highest-risk unknown in the Nigerian set, and an unknown lead time is an unbounded one.
- Ghana's Nursing and Midwifery Council provider accreditation route in its entirety — form, address,
  evidence, fee, lead time. Only the existence of "accredited CPD providers" is confirmed.
- Whether SQHN qualifies for MDCN's specialty-society rate of ₦200,000 rather than the independent-provider
  ₦1,000,000 plus transport for three officials. MDCN publishes no definition.
- Whether an entity whose chief executive is not a registered medical or dental practitioner can hold an
  MDCN provider licence at all. The requirement is published and unambiguous; whether it can be satisfied
  is a governance question for SQHN, not a paperwork question, and it needs checking today.
- Ghana AHPC's actual fee amounts, and which revenue-share bracket would apply.
- Whether a 2026 Ghanaian provider certification covers a January 2027 event, or whether a fresh 2027
  certification is required — the forms state a January-to-December certification period and are silent
  on carry-over.

## Recommended actions this week

Written enquiries, all of which produce planning inputs whatever the answer:

1. **Ghana MDC and AHPC** — does a live synchronous session count as an ordinary accredited event, outside
   the asynchronous online cap? This is the highest-value question in the file.
2. **Ghana AHPC** — which revenue-share bracket applies to an independently-run programme, and what are
   the actual fee amounts?
3. **Ghana Pharmacy Council** — what is the papers-closing date before the October 2026 Technical
   Committee sitting?
4. **Nigeria NMCN** — what is the lead time, and does the 2017 fee schedule still apply?
5. **Nigeria MDCN** — does SQHN qualify as a specialty society or association? And can a provider licence
   be held by an entity whose chief executive is not a registered practitioner?
6. **SQHN internally** — who is the chief executive of the entity that would hold the MDCN licence, and
   are they a registered medical or dental practitioner?

Item 6 is the one that could stop the Nigerian route dead, and it can be answered today without waiting
for anyone external.
